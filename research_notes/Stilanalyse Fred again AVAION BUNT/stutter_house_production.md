## 1. Definition and origin of stutter house (who coined it, when, defining artists and tracks) and how it relates to UK garage, 2-step, speed garage, breakbeat and emotional/melodic house

### Takeaway
Stutter house is a 2020s house microgenre that was named on TikTok and SoundCloud. It is defined by a single device: a gated, tremolo-like "stutter", meaning rapidly retriggered or volume-chopped vocal fragments, and also pads or synths, over a house groove. The name is credited to the Los Angeles curator Steve Cardigan (PutYouOn). His TikTok "Stutter House" playlist decodes to 13 Dec 2022. Fred again.. is credited with popularising the sound ("Marea (we've lost dancing)", "Danielle (smile on my face)", "Turn On The Lights again.."). BUNT., Lavern, Dezko, camoufly and AVAION are grouped with it. Sources cite deep house, future house and UK garage as its lineage, with Daft Punk's "Face to Face" and "Too Long" as precursors. Whether it is a genre at all is openly debated.

### Cited Findings
**Definition**
- "A contemporary house microgenre defined by the percussive use of chopped and rapidly re-triggered vocal fragments", over a four-on-the-floor groove, with roots in club-oriented house of the late 2010s — [Melodigging: Stutter House](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/stutter-house)
- Characterised by "the distinctive, tremolo chopping of vocal samples and pads". RYM ties the effect to trance-style gating and gives a typical tempo of about 125 BPM — [Rate Your Music: Stutter House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/stutter-house/) (via search snippet)
- Built around "a pulsating or stuttering vocal, lead or synth pad" — [Exron, "What is Stutter House?", 28 Dec 2023](https://exronmusic.com/2023/12/28/what-is-stutter-house/). Another explainer says the stutter "can be anything from a stuttering vocal to a stuttering synth or lead" — [TheBeatYouNeed](https://www.thebeatyouneed.com/genres/what-is-stutter-house) (attribution between Exron and TheBeatYouNeed uncertain)
- SoundCloud describes the stutter as a gated effect that turns vocals and percussion into overlapping melodic lines — [SoundCloud Stories: Stutter House](https://soundcloud.com/stories/post/stutter-house/) (via search snippet)
- Sugar Mode, the producer behind a Dropgun pack, in a promotional Splice interview: "Stutter House is a modern offshoot of house music that thrives on rhythmic vocal chops, dynamic grooves, and an infectious sense of movement. ## 2. Tempo, swing and drum programming (kick placement 4/4 vs. 2-step, shuffled hats, ghost snares, claps, breaks), with step grids

### Takeaway
Stutter-house sources give 124–130 BPM (often around 125–128) over a four-on-the-floor kick. The defining Fred again.. tracks span 123–134 BPM. The UKG-flavoured variant uses:
- a 2-step kick on beat 1 and halfway through beat 3 (step 11 of 16)
- snares or claps on 2 and 4
- swung 16th hats, usually 55–65% in MPC/Logic convention (50% = straight)
- off-beat open hats, each preceded by a closed hat one 16th earlier
- a ghost snare near the bar end
- occasionally dropping the downbeat kick of bar 2

**Swing maths (MPC/Logic style on 16ths).**
- Delay every even-numbered 16th by `(S/50 − 1) × T16`, where `T16 = 60 / BPM / 4`.
- At 130 BPM, T16 = 115.4 ms. The delay for each swing value:

  | Swing | Delay |
  |---|---|
  | 52% | 4.6 ms |
  | 55% | 11.5 ms |
  | 60% | 23.1 ms |
  | 65% | 34.6 ms |
  | 66.7% (triplet) | 38.5 ms |

- At 123 BPM, T16 = 122 ms; at 128 BPM, 117.2 ms; at 134 BPM, 111.9 ms.
- Apply swing to hats and percussion. Keep the kick (and usually the clap and bass) straight, per Lostaud and Orphiq.

**Velocity.** Use ±8–12 random variation on hats and 35–55 for ghost notes. These numbers are my inference; sources only say "lower velocity".

## 3. Vocal-chop and stutter technique in detail (slicing, grid sizes, gating, retriggers, pitch and formant shifting, reversing, the "re-re-re" stutter, placement, how the chop becomes the hook) and tools

### Takeaway
Two techniques are layered:
1. **Re-sequencing slices** into new short hook phrases, no longer than 1–2 bars. Slicing is done in Simpler, Fruity Slicer or on the timeline as audio. Slices are often pitched, reversed or formant-shifted.
2. **A tempo-synced volume gate or tremolo**: a square LFO at 1/16–1/32, a trance gate, ShaperBox or LFO Tool. It runs on both vocals and pads, plus "re-re-re" retrigger rolls that accelerate 1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32 into a phrase or drop.

Fred again.. himself mainly uses Logic with found-sound voice notes. Secondhand reports list stock Logic tools: Silver Compressor, Step FX and Retro Synth, plus Samplr Morph.

### Cited Findings
**Workflow and how the chop becomes the hook**
- Pick a short phrase, slice it, map the slices to a sampler and re-sequence them. Use dry takes for control. Unlicensed acapellas can get a track taken down — [Vocalfy](https://vocalfy.com/blog/how-to-make-vocal-chops)
- Fred again..'s vocal phrases are "deconstructed, processed and rearranged in a piecemeal way to create hooky, repetitive phrases". Keep chops short, no more than one or two bars — [MusicRadar: production tricks Fred again.. swears by](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by) or [MusicRadar build-along](https://www.musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples) (attribution between the two uncertain)
- Fred again..'s *Actual Life* series uses real-world audio samples and voice memos — [Wikipedia: Fred Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Again). ### Inferences
**MIDI and audio rules for the chop engine**
- **Grain grid:** 1/16 by default, with 1/8 and 1/32 variants. The hook phrase is 1–2 bars and loops. Slices are monophonic (each new note chokes the last, like Simpler Mono).
- **Gate:** a square wave on gain at 1/16 (sometimes 1/32), 100% depth, 50–60% duty cycle, with 2–5 ms ramps to avoid clicks. Apply it to the pad *and* the vocal bus. Rates:
  - 1/16 at 128 BPM = 117 ms period (8.53 Hz)
  - 1/32 at 128 BPM = 58.6 ms (17.1 Hz)
  - In general, a 1/16 rate in Hz = BPM / 15
- **Syncopated hook template** (one bar, 1/16 slices, 3+3+2 feel): hits on steps `1, 4, 7, 9, 12, 15`. Bar-2 variation: `1, 3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 11, 14`. Each hit's pitch is a chord tone (0, +3 or +4, +7, +12 semitones), with an occasional +12 octave jump. Rotate the vowel or slice index per hit.
- **"Re-re-re" roll** (last bar of a build):
  - Beats 1–2: 1/8 repeats (4 hits)
  - Beat 3: 1/16 (4 hits)
  - Beat 4: 1/32 (8 hits)
  - Ramp the gain from about 0.5 to 1.0 and open a lowpass or highpass as it goes. Optionally ramp pitch up to +12 st. Then the full word or phrase lands on the drop downbeat.
- **Pre-word stutter:** retrigger the first syllable on steps 13, 14, 15 and 16 of the bar before the phrase, then play the full word on the next downbeat.
- **"Answer" phrases:** tremolo plus bandpass (≈300 Hz–3 kHz "telephone") at −6 dB, per MusicRadar.
- **Variation:** reverse one slice in every 4–8; add a delay throw (1/8 dotted, feedback about 30%) at phrase ends. The specific values are inferences.

## 5. Bass (sub, reese, off-beat or 2-step patterns) and sidechain/pumping depth and shape

### Takeaway
Use a clean, mono, near-sine sub (about 40–120 Hz, fast attack, no modulation). Optionally add a separate mid-bass on top: a reese from 3 detuned oscillators, or an FM layer. Lock it to the kick:
- In 2-step, the bass follows the broken kick.
- In Afro house, the bass is a simple root pattern that moves at the end of the loop.

Pumping is everywhere in Fred-style tracks: pads, leads, vocals, percussion, even white noise are ducked by the kick. Use the fastest practical attack, set release from the kick's decay and the tempo, and use a sawtooth-like 1/4 duck shape.

### Cited Findings
- Near-sine, single-voice sub with a gentle low-pass around 80–120 Hz and a fast attack. Keep the mid-bass as a separate layer — [Lostaud](https://lostaud.io/blogs/production-blog/uk-bass-uk-garage-how-to-build-club-ready-grooves)
- Keep the sub simple (no modulation or effects) and layer it one or two octaves below the main bass — [Sample Focus](https://samplefocus.com/blog/how-to-produce-uk-garage-music/)
- Sub range 40–80 Hz; sidechain the bass to the kick — [Big Drum Records](https://www.bigdrumrecords.co.uk/blog/uk-garage-production-tips)
- A dark "reese" sub from three slightly detuned oscillators, plus a separate FM "wub" with a slow attack that suits the shuffled beat — [Studio Brootle](https://www.studiobrootle.com/uk-garage-drum-pattern-with-presets-and-bassline/)
- A garage bass in Massive starts from a sine sub tuned down two octaves and adds an FM-style layer — [Attack Magazine: Garage Bass](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/garage-bass/). See also [Attack: Subby Garage Bass](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/subby-garage-bass/)
- Afro house bass: "a simple G2 pattern that resolves to C3 and D3 at the end" (G minor) — [Native Instruments: Afro house production 101](https://blog.native-instruments.com/afro-house-production-101/)
- Amapiano is built on log drum plus sub. Afro house uses rolling, syncopated bass — [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-afro-house)
- Sidechain parameters — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4909894):
  - Threshold and ratio set how hard the mix ducks, "anything from a subtle rhythmic throb to extreme pumping"
  - Attack and release set how quickly it is pulled and pushed on each kick
  - "Too fast an attack will flatten and distort the mix; too slow and the effect is diluted"
- "Attack should be as fast as possible, though clicking may occur at the fastest plugin settings. The release is largely determined by the decay time of the kick and the bpm of the track" — [Attack Magazine](https://attackmagazine.com/technique/ten-production-tips-for-better-basslines/4)
- Daft Punk-style kick-triggered pumping in Logic, adjusting threshold and ratio to control how much the kick cuts through — [Point Blank](https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/blog/tutorial-daft-punk-style-vintage-sidechain-compression-in-logic-pro/)
- Volume-LFO ducking: sawtooth shape; 1/4 rate = pumping — [Computer Music via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/computer-music/20211130/282381222811242)
- Fred-style pumping is "applied liberally across pads, leads, and even vocal samples". Logic's Silver Compressor is reportedly used a lot for it — [Surge Sounds](https://surgesounds.com/post/what-genre-is-fred-again-recreating-his-signature-sound) (third-party)
- Build-along: white noise sidechained to the kick; percussion loops ducked under the 4/4 kick — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples)
- Stutter house has a "tight, sidechained low end" — [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/stutter-house) (attribution uncertain)

### Inferences
**Bass MIDI patterns** (16 steps, root of the current chord; octave 1–2, i.e., E1–E2 ≈ 41–82 Hz)
```
4/4 off-beat (stutter/Fred):    . . X . | . . X . | . . X . | . . X .     8th-length, sub sine
4/4 sustained + duck:           X———————————————————————————————          one note per chord, ducked by kick
2-step (follows kick):          X . . . | . . . X | . . X . | . X . .     step 1 long, 8 short pickup, 11 with kick, 14 short
Afro (NI-style, 4-bar loop):    bars 1–3: X . . X | . . X . | . . X . | . X . .   (root)
                                bar 4:    X . . X | . . . . | X . . . | X . . .   (root, root, iv, v → e.g. G2, G2, C3, D3)
```

**Sidechain envelope** (Web Audio GainNode, retriggered on every kick)
- Drop to `1 − depth` within 2–5 ms.
- Recover over about 55–70% of a beat. At 128 BPM a beat is 469 ms, so the release is about 260–330 ms. Use a linear or slightly exponential ramp, giving the sawtooth "pump" shape.
- Depth: pads and vocals −8 to −12 dB; percussion loops and noise −6 to −10 dB; sub −10 dB to full duck at onset (kick and sub overlap).

All of these numbers are inferences; no source gives numeric depths.

- **Soft "felt/upright" keys** (no source names the instrument):
  - 2-operator FM, ratio 1:1, with the modulation index decaying 3 → 0.5 over about 300 ms, plus a quiet 2:1 partial
  - Lowpass at 2.5–4 kHz; amp decay 1.5–3 s with sustain 0
  - Tape wow: pitch LFO at 0.5–1 Hz, ±5–10 cents
  - A noise bed (pink noise, highpass 1 kHz) at about −40 dB
  - Alternative: a sampled piano with a lowpass at about 3 kHz and soft saturation
- **Pad:** 2–3 detuned saws (±7–12 cents), lowpass 1.5–3 kHz, attack 200–400 ms, release 1–2 s. Then apply the 1/16 gate (Q3) and sidechain (Q5). Optional reverb send with 4–8 s decay and the low end cut below about 300 Hz (per MusicRadar's "tame the low end").
- **Pluck:** saw or square through a lowpass whose envelope decays in 100–200 ms. It plays 16ths on the current chord's bass note, an octave or two up.
- **Riser:** white noise through a bandpass (Q ≈ 2) sweeping 200 Hz → 8 kHz over 8–16 bars, with gain rising and the reverb tail growing. For a **downer**, reverse the sweep (8 kHz → 200 Hz over 1–2 bars) after the drop. The noise is ducked by the kick.
- **Kick:** a sine with a pitch envelope 150 → 50 Hz over 40–80 ms and amp decay 250–400 ms, plus a click transient (1–3 ms noise burst). Run it through a `WaveShaperNode` (tanh, drive 2–4) for "slightly distorted, sub-forward".
- **Vinyl crackle:** sparse random impulses (Poisson rate 10–30 per second, random amplitude) through a highpass at 1–2 kHz, plus low-level filtered noise and a 33⅓ rpm (0.55 Hz) wow.
- **"Bleeping" Afro-electro arp (WACUKA-like):** a square or triangle blip with a 30–60 ms decay playing 16th-note figures, pitched to minor pentatonic or chord tones. The description is from press copy; the patch values are invented.

## 7. Arrangement conventions (long build-ups, filtered intros, breakdown with vocals, drop behaviour, energy curves)

### Takeaway
Sections come in multiples of 8 bars. Club versions have DJ-friendly intros of 16–32 bars. The breakdown strips layers and *teases* the vocal hook. The build accelerates the stutter (1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32) under filter sweeps and noise risers. A short stripped pause precedes the drop, and the drop delivers the *complete* chopped hook on the full groove. No source analyses actual bar counts of Fred again.., BUNT. or AVAION tracks.

### Cited Findings
- "Tease the full vocal hook in the breakdown, then deliver the complete stutter pattern on the drop". Filtering and noise risers lead into the drop. Minimal arrangements keep the vocal in focus — [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/stutter-house) (via search summary)
- Stutter as a build tool: 8th → 16th → 32nd — [Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/tips-for-better-buildups-and-drops-in-edm/)
- The breakdown strips back layers, and a "stripped" pause comes just before the full groove returns — [Wikipedia: Breakdown (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakdown_(music))
- Intros are often 16–32 bars, setting the mood with percussion, ambience or vocal samples. The drop is the energy peak, with a catchy bassline and driving rhythm — [Breve Music Studios](https://brevemusicstudios.com/article/structuring-your-house-track-for-maximum-impact/)
- Intros give DJs a clean section to mix in — [ILX forum thread](https://ilxor.com/ILX/ThreadSelectedControllerServlet?boardid=41&threadid=21272) (attribution uncertain)
- Common template: intro, build-up, drop, breakdown, outro, in 8-bar multiples — [Myloops](https://www.myloops.net/ultimate-guide-to-progressive-house-track-structure-and-flow)
- WACUKA runs 3:10 per SongBPM, and an "Extended Version" exists for DJs — [SongBPM](https://songbpm.com/@avaion/wacuka-hm5qt), [Beatport](https://www.beatport.com/track/wacuka/19888611)
- Answer phrases are thinned (tremolo, telephone EQ, lower volume), which gives call-and-response inside sections — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/tech/how-to-make-vocal-stutter-effects-in-your-daw-394649)

### Inferences
**Radio-length template.** About 3:00–3:30. At 124–132 BPM that is roughly 96–112 bars: 3:10 at 130 BPM ≈ 103 bars, and 3:00 at 128 BPM = 96 bars.

| Section | Bars | Content | Energy (0–1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8 | lowpassed pad (cutoff 400 Hz → 2 kHz), vinyl/field-recording bed, one ungated vocal fragment | 0.25 |
| Groove A | 16 | kick + hats + bass; sparse chops (half the hook) | 0.55 |
| Breakdown | 16 | drums out (or shaker only); piano/pad and more natural vocal; hook teased | 0.35 |
| Build | 8 | stutter roll 1/8 → 1/32 in the final bar; highpass sweep on music bus 60 Hz → 600 Hz; noise riser; optional clap roll 1/4 → 1/8 → 1/16 | 0.4 → 0.8 |
| Pause | ½–1 beat (optionally 1 bar) | silence, or reverb tail only | 0.1 |
| Drop | 16 | full gated pad + complete chop hook + pumping sidechain + full bass | 1.0 |
| Bridge / Breakdown 2 | 8 | new vocal line or stripped chords | 0.4 |
| Drop 2 | 16 | variation (hook pitched up an octave, extra percussion, new chop order) | 1.0 |
| Outro | 8 | elements removed one by one | 0.3 |

**Club or extended version:** add 16–32 bars of drums-only intro and outro, plus a full 32-bar breakdown.

## 8. Afro house conventions for comparison (tempo; log drum, conga, shaker and tom patterns; chord and vocal conventions) and how melodic Afro house (Keinemusik-style, "WACUKA") differs

### Takeaway
Afro house sits at about 110–125 BPM, most often around 118–124. It has a straight four-on-the-floor kick under swung 16th percussion (shakers, congas, bongos, djembes). Its other conventions:
- "answering" percussion pairs and 3-against-4 or 6-against-4 cross-rhythms
- tom loops filling the low mids (low end rolled off around 80 Hz, sidechained)
- one-chord minor grooves or i–VII–VI–iv progressions
- simple root bass
- log drums push a track toward amapiano

Melodic Afro house (Keinemusik, &ME, Rampa) adds a piano harmonic core, tonal percussion (bells, glass) acting as melody, "trippy, uplifting" vocals and a clean low end.

WACUKA (AVAION & Sofiya Nzau, 10 Jan 2025) is faster than the Afro-house norm at **130 BPM** in **A minor / C major** (relative keys). Press describes it as Afro-soul vocals over electro-house energy with "bleeping rhythms". Its tempo is closer to stutter house and UKG.

### Cited Findings
**Afro house: tempo and groove**
- Native Instruments — [Afro house production 101](https://blog.native-instruments.com/afro-house-production-101/):
  - Sets its project to 122 BPM; "Afro house typically ranges between 110 and 125 BPM", with 118–122 often preferred
  - Keys: a single-chord groove in G minor using extended chords
  - Bass: G2 resolving to C3 and D3
- Attack "Deep Afro House" — [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/deep-afro-house/):
  - 122–125 BPM; **swing "80–85"**, unusually high. Attack's convention is that 50% = straight; see the inference below on how to read it.
  - Sounds: djembes, congas, bongos
  - A bouncy, jacking kick and snare pattern; a deep, mid-range-rich kick with longer decay
  - Hi-hat and tambourine interplay creates the shuffle, with the tambourine a tight sample low in the mix
  - The example percussion is a short, lightly reverbed bongo hit; a low off-beat synth adds the final layer
- Orphiq — [What Is Afro House?](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-afro-house):
  - About 115–125 BPM with a straight four-on-the-floor kick
  - Layers: 16th-note shakers, a conga or tom figure answering the kick, a longer-cycle rattle or bell
  - "Program two parts that answer each other: shaker against rattle, conga against tom, bell against clap"
  - "Run a conga or shaker figure in a six-against-four feel while the kick stays straight, and the bar starts to rotate rather than march"
  - "Apply a small amount of swing to the 16th-note percussion and leave the kick, clap, and bass on the grid. Enough that you feel it, not enough that a DJ fights it"
  - A log drum "pulls the track toward amapiano"
- The shaker often matters more than the clap for forward drive. Straight 16ths can work, but full quantisation sounds stiff, so add slight timing shifts and lower velocity on the in-between notes — [IQ Sounds](https://iqsounds.com/blogs/news/afro-house-drum-programming-guide)
- Black Coffee-style tutorial — [The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/how-to-make-afro-house-like-black-coffee-complete-tutorial):
  - A tom loop under the kick "sets the groove and rhythm while filling the lower-mid frequencies"; its low end is rolled off around 80 Hz and Kickstart sidechain ducks it on every kick
  - A noisy shaker with varied MIDI velocities, plus a second shaker playing one hit at the end of every bar
  - A high wood shot with reverb
  - A tom shot on bars 2 and 4
- A short conga phrase answers a rim hit, and a shaker fill leads into the clap — [Hot Groove Samples](https://hotgroovesamples.com/blogs/news/afro-house-production-club-ready-groove)
- Future Proof Music School — [Future Proof](https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/afro-house-production-a-producer-s-guide-to-crafting-deep-soulful-grooves):
  - "3 against 4" shaker-versus-clave patterns; swing or triplet timing on percussion
  - Keinemusik's "Send Return" as a reference
  - A minor-key **1–7–6–4** progression (i–VII–VI–iv); common keys D, A, G and E minor
- "3-step" Afro house uses three kicks per 4/4 bar — [Time To House](https://timetohouse.com/en/articles/what-is-3-step-afro-house). An amapiano/3-step guide describes the kick on the first and third beats with the snare or clap in between — [Afroplug](https://afroplug.com/how-to-make-3-step-amapiano-beats/) (summary wording ambiguous)
- Amapiano is built around wide log-drum basslines and deep, low-pitched kicks — [Wikipedia: Amapiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amapiano)

**Melodic Afro house (Keinemusik)**
- Producer notes on Keinemusik's "More Love" (Rampa, &ME, to a degree Moderat) — [Production Music Live](https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/producer-notes-keinemusik-more-love-rampa-me-afro-house-moderat-style-ableton):

## 9. Recreating the "vocal chop" timbre with royalty-free material or synthesis (ooh/aah vowel samples, choir samples, formant synthesis) and what tutorials say works

### Takeaway
There are two routes:
- **(a) Licensed or royalty-free vocals**, chopped as in Q3: sample packs, NI's free vocal samples, or your own recorded "ooh/aah" vowels.
- **(b) Synthesised vowels.** Pass a harmonically rich source (a saw with light unison, or a pulse/click train) through 2–3 parallel band-pass filters at formant frequencies. F1 and F2 matter most, and the filters' Q and gain matter as well as their frequencies. Follow with a lowpass around 4 kHz. Switching formant sets per chop gives the "syllable" effect.

Reference values (male averages, CCRMA): "ee" ≈ 270/2290/3010 Hz; "ah" ≈ 730/1090/2440 Hz.

### Cited Findings
- From-scratch Serum 2 vowel: "vowels are nothing but resonant peaks called formants... recreate those peaks and the ear fills in a voice". One saw oscillator with light unison (3 voices, detune about 0.08–0.12) feeds **two band-pass filters in parallel** — [Monosounds: Serum 2 Vocal Chops](https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-vocal-chops/)
- Serum's Formant I/II/III filter types are each tuned for different vowel transitions — [PausePlayRepeat](https://sounds.pauseplayrepeat.com/blogs/news/understanding-serums-filters). Modulating a formant filter's cutoff acts as a vowel selector — [Monosounds: Serum 2 growls](https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-dubstep-growls/)
- Put a low-pass around 4 kHz after a formant filter to tame sibilant fizz — [Monosounds: Serum 2 filters](https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-filters-explained/)
- Formant values (F1/F2/F3, male averages): **"ee" 270 / 2290 / 3010 Hz** and **"ah" 730 / 1090 / 2440 Hz** — [CCRMA, Stanford](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~kglee/m220c/formant.html)
- F1 and F2 do most of the work of identifying a vowel. Front vowels have higher F2; low vowels have higher F1 — [Wikipedia: Formant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formant) (via search summary)
- Formant synthesis method: a click train standing in for glottal pulses, passed through band-pass filters. One synthesis model sets each bandwidth to about 10% of its formant frequency — [IIIT Hyderabad Virtual Lab](https://ssp-iiith.vlabs.ac.in/exp/formant-synthesis/theory.html) (attribution of the 10% figure uncertain)
- "It's not just the centre frequencies of the formants that affect the quality of the sound, but also the narrowness of their pass-bands (their Qs) and their gains" — [Reason Studios: Thor formant filters](https://reasonstudios.com/news/post/thor-demystified-17-filters-pt-5-formant-filters)
- **Conflict:** Unison suggests about 300 Hz + 1000 Hz for "ah" and about 300 + 2200 Hz for "ee" — [Unison](https://unison.audio/formant-filters/). This contradicts CCRMA's "ah" F1 of 730 Hz; Unison's "ah" values look more like a back rounded vowel ("oh/oo").
- A vocal synth (vocoder, formant synth or vocal-modelled instrument) makes a voice-like tone with no recording. Output's Exhale is mentioned for vocal one-shots, loops and phrases — [Voxalized](https://voxalized.com/blog/vocal-chops)
- Without samples: modulate a resonant filter with high-frequency waveforms at high resonance. The poster adds that formant sounds are "tricky" without samples — [Vintage Synth Explorer forum](https://forum.vintagesynth.com/viewtopic.php?t=76876)
- Free vocal samples for chopping — [Native Instruments: Vocal chops 101](https://blog.native-instruments.com/vocal-chops/). Seek permission before sampling commercial records — [FaderPro](https://blog.faderpro.com/techniques/how-to-create-vocal-chops/). Unlicensed acapellas risk takedowns — [Vocalfy](https://vocalfy.com/blog/how-to-make-vocal-chops)
- Royalty-free stutter-house packs: [Loopmasters "Stutter House" (125 BPM)](https://www.loopmasters.com/genres/25-House/products/18114-Stutter-House), [Dropgun "Vocal Stutter House"](https://splice.com/sounds/packs/dropgun-samples/vocal-stutter-house/samples), [Dropgun "Vocal Stutter House 2"](https://splice.com/sounds/packs/dropgun-samples/vocal-stutter-house-2/story)
- Use different vowel sounds across chops to avoid a robotic result — [The Vocal Market](https://thevocalmarket.com/blogs/how-to/how-to-chop-vocals-like-a-pro-any-daw)
- MusicRadar's Fred-style build-along used a royalty-free Ableton factory-library "choppy vocal loop" in place of found recordings — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples)

### Inferences
**Web Audio "vowel-chop voice"** (all parameter values are inferences)
- **Source:**
  - 3 sawtooth `OscillatorNode`s (detune −10 / 0 / +10 cents), mirroring Monosounds' light 3-voice unison
  - Or a `PeriodicWave` with harmonic amplitudes falling about 1/n²–1/n^1.5, which is closer to a glottal source
  - Vibrato: 5–5.5 Hz, ±10–20 cents, fading in after about 150 ms
- **Formant bank:** 3 parallel `BiquadFilterNode` set to "bandpass", summed.
  - Q ≈ f / bandwidth, using the 10% rule, so Q ≈ 10. Example: F1 730 Hz → Q 10 → 73 Hz bandwidth.
  - Gains: F1 0 dB, F2 −6 dB, F3 −12 dB.
  - Then a 4 kHz lowpass, then the amplitude envelope (attack 5–10 ms), then the 1/16 gate.
- **Vowels:** use "ah" (730/1090/2440) and "ee" (270/2290/3010) from CCRMA. For "oo" and "oh", see Gaps.
- **Syllables:** for each chop, pick a vowel and glide the filter frequencies over 20–40 ms (`setTargetAtTime`) to fake diphthongs ("ah→ee", "oh→ah"). Add a 5–15 ms band-passed noise burst (2–6 kHz, about −20 dB) at the onset as a pseudo-consonant ("t/k/h").
- **"Ooh/aah" choir pad:** 4–6 such voices, detuned ±15 cents, attack 150–300 ms, with a long reverb. Gate and sidechain it like the pad.
- **Melody lane:** chop pitches on chord tones, with an occasional +12 jump. Rotate the vowel per slice, and occasionally play a slice reversed (amplitude envelope inverted: slow attack, abrupt end).
- **Licensing:** for copyright safety in the browser, prefer the synthesised voice plus self-recorded or CC0 vowels. Commercial-pack licences vary and were not checked.
