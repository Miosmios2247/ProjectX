## 1. Which 10–15 tracks are most popular or representative, and what are their BPM and key?

### Takeaway
By streams, the core canon is "Marea", "Turn On The Lights again..", "Delilah", "Jungle" and "Rumble", followed by "adore u", "leavemealone", "ten", "Billie" and "places to be". The hits sit almost entirely between 123 and 140 BPM. A separate 174 BPM drum & bass strand starts in 2023. The tempo rises by era: about 112–128 house (2019–21), then 130–134 garage-house (2022), then 140 grime/dubstep/UKG and 174 d&b (2023–25). Key databases keep disagreeing about relative major versus minor for the biggest tracks. That disagreement itself points to tonally ambiguous loops (see Q2).

## 2. Chord progressions, harmonic rhythm, loop length, voicing and modal colour

### Takeaway
The recognisable harmony is not triadic I–V–vi–IV. In most of the analysable hits, the loop is built on a major-seventh chord on scale degree ♭6 of the minor key (equivalently IVmaj7 of the relative major). Marea, Delilah, ten, adore u, Kyle and Turn On The Lights again.. all contain this VImaj7/IVmaj7. Around it sit minor-seventh chords and an aeolian VI–VII–i ascent, usually with no V→I resolution. The minor and bass-led tracks (Jungle, Kammy, leavemealone) reduce harmony to two- or three-chord aeolian cells or near-static drones.

- **Fingerprint 1, the "VImaj7 / IVmaj7 anchor".** Six of the eleven tracks analysed above contain a maj7 chord on ♭6 of the minor key (Marea D♭maj7, Delilah E♭maj7, ten Gmaj7, adore u Fmaj7, Kyle A♭maj7, TOTL Dmaj7). "Jungle" arguably joins them, since C♯m over an A bass is an Amaj7 sound. The VI chord in some quality appears in all the others as well: Kammy E♭(7), leavemealone F, Danielle B♭7, Billie E♭. The loops tend to *start* on this chord. A generator should make "VImaj7 of the minor = IVmaj7 of the major" the default first chord and the colour chord.
- **Fingerprint 2, two loop archetypes beginning on that chord:**
  - (A) **Aeolian ascent:** VI(maj7) → VII → i(7) → (III / iv7 / v). This covers Marea, adore u and Kammy.
  - (B) **Minor-seventh fall:** VImaj7 → i7 → v7 → (i or IIImaj7). This covers Delilah (2022) and ten (2024), which have essentially the same progression transposed (Delilah Gm, ten Bm).
  - Neither archetype is I–V–vi–IV. Marea's IV–V–vi–I is a different order (stepwise rising bass IV → V → vi), not a rotation.
- **Fingerprint 3, minor v7 instead of a dominant V.** Delilah (Dm7 in Gm), ten (F♯m7 in Bm) and Kammy (Dm in Gm) use a minor v. TOTL and leavemealone use plagal iv → i. A raised leading tone or V7→i cadence is atypical. Klang's C♯ major for TOTL is the only hint of one, and it is unverified.
- **Fingerprint 4, tonic ambiguity.** Because loops start on IV/VI and resolve weakly, databases split between relative keys (Q1). The generator should not resolve to I at the end of each loop. Let the loop "hang" and let the vocal sample imply the centre.
- **Bass-led and riff tracks (Jungle, Kammy, Rumble, leavemealone):** harmony shrinks to a drone or two or three chords. Kammy's most-used chord is a bare root; Jungle is mostly C♯m bars. The generator needs a "drone / two-chord cell" mode in addition to four-chord loops.
- **Voicing:** the 7ths (maj7 on VI/IV, m7 on i/vi/iii/v) are part of the identity, so render them as 7th chords, not triads. Danielle's "B♭7", whose A♭ is ♭3 of F, may be a transcription artefact or a blues/gospel IV7 colour. It is unverified.
- **Harmonic rhythm and loop length (best guess, not sourced):** with four chords and tempos of 123–134 BPM, one chord per bar in four-bar loops (sometimes two bars per chord) matches dance convention. Treat this as an adjustable parameter, not a fact.

- No chord data was found for Rumble, places to be, Victory Lap, baby again.., London or solo.
- No source documents voicing style: gospel voicings, spread versus close position, sus chords, or inversions. Whether he plays block chords or arpeggiates (Tiny Desk "looped piano keys") is unknown.
- Chord *order* in Chordify/ChordU lists is not confirmed except for the Marea chorus.

## 3. Drums and groove

### Takeaway
"Four-on-the-floor emotional house" describes only one strand, mainly Marea and Billie at 123–128 BPM. The evidence shows four templates by tempo:
- 123–128 house / "stutter house";
- 130–135 garage-house / speed-garage with break-derived swing and unorthodox double-16th kicks;
- 140 "stuttering", dubstep-infused or grime beats;
- 174 drum & bass.

The kits are 909-based and processed with drum-bus saturation and reverb. Live, the drums are finger-drummed on pads.

### Cited Findings
- **Attack Magazine "Beat Dissected" in the style of "Kammy (like i do)":**
  - The kick programming is described as "slightly unorthodox". It uses a 909 kick sample ("Kick 909 Tune 4" from the Skitter and Step pack) and lowers the velocity of the first kick "whenever two sixteenth-note kicks fall in a row".
  - The hat is a closed 909 from Ableton's Core Library.
  - Swing comes from a groove extracted from a funky break loop and applied to the drum MIDI.
  - The drum channel runs through Drum Buss and Reverb.
  - Context given: Fred's finger drumming over KAMILLE's vocal in his Boiler Room set.
  - Sources: [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/breaking-down-how-to-make-beats-inspired-by-fred-again/)
- **MusicRadar "make a track in the style of Fred Again.. and DJ Seinfeld" (tutorial, not Fred's own process):**
  - Four-on-the-floor kick with layered percussion.
  - Shaker and tambourine layer sidechained to the kick.
  - An 808-style bass loop ducked against the kick.
  - Risers, downers and white noise sidechained to the kick.
  - Source: [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples)
- **Genre and beat descriptors by track:**

### Inferences
- **Templates for the generator:**
  - **T1, 122–128 "stutter house":** 4/4 909 kick, offbeat open hat, clap on 2 and 4, shaker or tambourine 16ths ducked by the kick.
  - **T2, 130–135 garage-house / speed garage:** 909 kick mostly on quarters, plus occasional pickup double 16ths (for example the last 16th of a beat into the next downbeat, first hit at lower velocity, per Attack). Closed 909 hats. Swing taken from a break groove, not a uniform MPC swing value.
  - **T3, 138–140 half-time / "stuttering" grime-dubstep:** kick on 1, snare on 3, stuttered fills.
  - **T4, 172–176 d&b:** two-step breakbeat (on a 16-step grid: kick on step 1 and step 11, i.e. the "and" of 3; snare on steps 5 and 13, i.e. beats 2 and 4).
  - Exact step grids are my extrapolation; only the double-16th kick and break-groove swing are sourced.
- "Finger-drummed" live versions mean slight timing looseness and velocity variation are authentic. Humanise drums (timing jitter of a few ms, velocity ±10–20%), rather than producing a rigid grid.
- The Daft Punk "Revolution 909" sample in "Jungle" and the 909 kits used in tutorials point to 909 as the default kit colour.

- Whether drums typically enter late after long vocal or piano intros is not confirmed by any source. Only "Delilah"'s vocal-first intro is documented (Q8).
- No source on distorted or clipped kicks, sampled breaks (other than groove extraction) or hat shuffle percentages.

## 4. Bass

### Takeaway
Bass is either a lo-fi sampled noise source re-pitched into a line, as in "Jungle" (made from mains hum), or a heavily distorted Reese with a sine sub in the 135–140 BPM tracks (Rumble-type). It is ducked against the kick. Tutorials describe simple two-note lines.

### Cited Findings
- **"Jungle" bass:** made from mains hum picked up through a faulty or "dodgy" cable. It was recorded lo-fi (the tutorial mentions iPhone Voice Memos), then "chopped-up, processed and re-pitched on the Logic Pro timeline" into the main bassline — [MusicTech tutorial](https://musictech.com/tutorials/tips/how-to-recreate-fred-again-s-jungle-bass-sound/), [MusicTech news](https://musictech.com/news/music/fred-again-reveals-bass-sound-jungle-dodgy-cable-tiktok/), [MusicRadar news](https://www.musicradar.com/news/fred-again-jungle-bass-sound-dodgy-audio-cable). The tutorial notes that UK/EU mains hum is 50 Hz, recommends loading the recording into a sampler (TAL-Sampler) with loop points, and says to "keep it lo-fi" — [MusicTech tutorial](https://musictech.com/tutorials/tips/how-to-recreate-fred-again-s-jungle-bass-sound/)
- **Attack Magazine "Fred Again inspired Reese basslines with Twin 3"** (inspired by Chase & Status and Fred again..):
  - 135 BPM, "a simple two-note monophonic bassline".
  - Mono mode with 64 unison voices and 10% spread.
  - Heavily distorted, so gain-staging matters from the start.
  - Four oscillators: a sine one octave down as the sub, with detuned saws on top.
  - Source: [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/fred-again-inspired-reese-basslines-with-twin-3/)
- **Logic ES2 (secondary source):** he often starts bass from a sine wave in ES2 — [Melodics](https://melodics.com/blog/what-daw-does-fred-again-use)
- **Style tutorial:** an 808-style bass loop ducked against the kick — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples)

### Inferences
- Generator bass modes:
  - (a) Root-following sub (sine) that holds chord roots, mainly in the house templates.
  - (b) Two-note Reese ostinato (root plus ♭6 or ♭7, or an octave) with distortion, for the 135–140 templates.
  - (c) "Sample-bass": a short noisy one-shot re-pitched per note.
  - Duck all of them with kick-keyed sidechain.
- A lot of the identity comes from the *timbre* (hum, distortion, lo-fi), so MIDI alone only gives the note skeleton.

### Gaps
- No transcribed bass patterns (rhythm or notes) for Marea, Delilah, TOTL, Rumble or leavemealone.
- No source on the exact sidechain depth or release times Fred uses.

## 5. Vocals: found samples, chopping, pitching, hook role

### Takeaway
The vocal is the identity, and it is real human recording rather than a synthesized formant chop. Sources include speech and voicemails (Marea), friends' and artists' phone videos of live singing (Delilah, Danielle), Instagram stories, YouTube and FaceTime clips, and rap acapellas from the guest's own catalogue (leavemealone). These are chopped, re-pitched and re-sequenced so that the sample carries the hook. Tracks are often named after the person sampled, with a phrase from the sample in brackets.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Naming convention:** Marea (Marea Stamper), Delilah (Delilah Montagu), Kammy (KAMILLE), Bleu (Yung Bleu) and Danielle (070 Shake) follow a "Name (phrase from the sample)" format. The 070 Shake/Danielle name link is my inference and is not stated in the sources.
- For a generator, the vocal lane should be modelled as **sample slices triggered by MIDI**, not as a synth. Take one recorded phrase (speech or a sung line, ideally phone-recorded with room noise) and cut it into 4–12 syllable slices. Re-sequence them into a one- or two-bar hook with repeats or stutters of one syllable (8th/16th retriggers at phrase ends). Re-pitch the slices to chord tones, with whole-sample pitch-up as in Rumble's bridge.
- Let the vocal start the track alone or over a sparse piano (Delilah).
- The earlier "synthesized formant vocal chops" approach fails on the most important axis. The source material is natural, intimate, lo-fi voice (speech intonation, phone compression, room tone). In leavemealone and Delilah the chop rhythm follows the words, not a fixed grid.
- Spoken-word samples (Marea) suggest a "speech hook" mode: a spoken sentence fragment looped and pitched to fit the key, used as the lead melody.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives Fred's exact chopping workflow (Logic sampler type, slice lengths, retrigger rates), pitch-shift amounts, or whether he uses formant shifting. No source mentioned formant processing at all.
- Whether the chopped vocal forms a 16th-note stutter hook in specific tracks (Marea, TOTL, Delilah) is unverified. The "stutter house" label exists (RYM on adore u), but the search budget ran out before its definition could be researched.

- **Suggested arrangement grammar for the generator** (reconstruction, not sourced bar counts):
  1. Intro: found vocal or speech alone, or over room tone and a sparse piano loop (4–8 bars).
  2. Build: piano/7th-chord loop plus reverb, then muted or filtered drums or percussion, with sidechain depth rising.
  3. Main or "drop" section: the full drum template, with the vocal chopped into a repeating hook and the same 4-chord loop. The emphasis is on rhythm and texture, not a new melody.
  4. Breakdown: the drums drop out and the vocal returns un-chopped (the "true" sentence), often the emotional peak.
  5. Second main section with an added layer (extra chop variation, octave piano, choir-like reverb).
  6. Short outro back to raw audio.
  - Total about 3–4 minutes.
  - The harmonic loop stays the same throughout. Contrast comes from *subtraction and addition* of layers and from the vocal's state (raw versus chopped versus pitched).
- "Boiler Room energy" for a generator means pad-performed variations: re-triggered vocal stabs, fills and mutes. Expose these as performance or randomisation parameters, not fixed arrangement.
