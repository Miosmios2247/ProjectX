## 1. Era timeline: who made what, and when (needed to keep the eras apart)

### Takeaway
BUNT. was a Stuttgart duo (Levi Wijk + Nico Crispin) from 2014. It has been Wijk's solo project since Crispin left in May 2021. The real stylistic break came later, around Dec 2022/Jan 2023. In 2022 the solo project was still releasing folk house ("Folktales"). Then train-made TikTok snippets led to "Clouds" (Jan 2023, Arista), and from that point the sound is sample-based stutter house. 2025–26 added big co-writes and features (Tom Odell, The Chainsmokers/Izzy Bizu, Malou, RØAM), festival main stages, and the 15-track album "Levi Did It" (23 Oct 2026).

### Inferences
- For style modelling, put the era boundary at Jan 2023 ("Clouds"), not at May 2021 (Crispin's exit), because the solo project still released folk house in 2022 ("Folktales").
- The current era has two sub-phases. In 2023–24 he flipped existing vocals into stutter house ("Clouds", "Lost My Mind", "Lose It All", "Peace In Silence", "Moment"), plus one DnB-tempo hit ("TRIPPIN"). In 2025–26 the records are bigger co-writes and features ("Best Day of My Life" with Tom Odell, "Spaces", "i need u", "Nothing Lasts Forever").
- Conflict: Wikipedia's "deep house" label is not echoed by any other source found. The labels actually used for the current catalogue are "stutter house" (RYM, remake vendors, RA, press), "drum & bass" (TRIPPIN) and "future garage" (Lost My Mind). Treat "deep house" as loose wording.
- "One Two Police" with Mo-Do is very probably a flip of Mo-Do's 1994 Eurodance hit "Eins, Zwei, Polizei". That would fit his practice of re-contextualising existing songs, but it is unverified.

### Gaps
- Wikipedia could not be read in full, so the label chronology and the early discography with years are only partly confirmed.
- No complete tracklist or credits for "Levi Did It" yet; the album is not out until 23 Oct 2026.

### Inferences
- **Tempo model for a generator:** default 128–133 BPM (Clouds 130, Best Day 130, You 130, Moment 127–130, I Will Find You 127, Spaces 132, Lost My Mind 133). Second mode at 140 (Lose It All, Peace In Silence). A rarer but very popular **174–175 DnB mode** (TRIPPIN, his No. 2 track).
- **Mode model:** house-tempo hits lean minor (Clouds and Lost My Mind in A minor, Lose It All in B minor, Spaces in B♭ minor, possibly Crazy in F♯ minor). Major keys show up on the "uplifting" records: TRIPPIN in D, Best Day in D♭, Peace In Silence in F♯/G♭. Two tracks (You, Moment) have unresolved major/minor readings. In both cases the conflicting readings are relative keys or nearby keys, typical of loop-based harmony with an ambiguous tonic.
- Beatport's 131 BPM for TRIPPIN is almost exactly 3/4 × 175 (131.25). One possibility is that the analysis locked onto a dotted (3-against-4) rhythmic layer. Either way, ~175 is supported by four sites, by remake vendors' "Drum & Bass" label and by reviews (Section 4). This is speculative.
- Lost My Mind went from Duhé's 103 BPM / B minor to 133 BPM / A minor (+29 % tempo, −2 semitones). Best Day went from 118 BPM / B♭ to 130 BPM / D♭ (+10 % tempo, +3 semitones). Simple varispeed cannot produce these pairs, so the borrowed vocals were apparently time-stretched and pitch-shifted independently (or partly re-sung). A generator's vocal-sample module therefore needs independent pitch and time control.
- Radio edits are short: 2:10–3:05 (Peace In Silence 2:10, BELOHNEN 2:10, Lose It All 2:19, Lost My Mind ~2:24, Clouds 2:38, Crazy 2:43, Best Day 2:50, You 2:54, Moment 3:05). Extended mixes exist separately (Section 9).

The available chord data, all auto-detected or crowd-sourced, shows **very short loops of 2–4 chords**. ## 4. Drums and groove

### Takeaway
The evidence points to three groove families rather than one. First, a steady 4/4 house-tempo groove (127–133, sometimes 140) labelled stutter house. Second, a garage-flavoured variant: "Lost My Mind (Wake Up)" is sold by three remake vendors as "Future Garage". Third, a drum & bass groove at ~175 BPM ("TRIPPIN"). Exact kick, hat and percussion patterns and swing values are **not documented anywhere I could reach**.

- Genre-level descriptions, not BUNT.-specific:
  - A stutter-house sample pack describes "sliced, gated, and pitch-bent" vocal fragments over "bouncy, Eurodance-flavored basslines and 4/4 drums with claps and hats". — [Rewind Samples (marketing copy)](https://rewindsamples.gumroad.com/l/refract)
  - A genre guide recommends about 124–130 BPM, 4/4 with a solid four-on-the-floor kick, and crisp, uncluttered drums "so the vocal stutter can sit front-and-center". — likely [Melodigging: Stutter House](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/stutter-house) (search-summary attribution, unverified)

### Inferences
- **House mode:** 4/4 four-on-the-floor kick, clap/snare on beats 2 and 4, open or offbeat hats, and sidechain-style pumping. This fits "steady groove", "head-nodding bounce", the genre descriptions and the remakers' Kickstart requirement. It is **not verified track by track**. The "oscillating beats" wording suggests rhythmic amplitude modulation (stutter/tremolo) on non-drum layers too.
- **Garage mode (Lost My Mind):** 133 BPM with a shuffled 2-step/garage pattern (kick not on all four beats, swung hats, syncopated snares) is consistent with the "Future Garage" label, but that label comes from commercial remakers.
- **DnB mode (TRIPPIN):** ~175 BPM breakbeat with two-step snares on 2 and 4 of the half-time bar. If the generator offers it, it should still carry the same vocal-hook logic; RYM tags TRIPPIN as both DnB and stutter house.
- **Difference from the early era:** early tracks were 124–127 BPM festival/progressive-house four-on-the-floor (Kontor/Interscope) or ~95–100 BPM folk-pop. Current tracks are faster (127–140) and more varied (garage, DnB), and the drums serve a vocal-chop hook instead of a folk-instrument lead.

### Gaps
- No transcription, tutorial or interview gives BUNT.'s actual drum patterns, hat programming, swing percentage, percussion layers, fills or drum sample choices. YouTube remake videos exist (Section 11) but could not be viewed.

## 6. Vocals: who sings, how they are sampled, and how the "stutter" works

### Takeaway
This is the core of the current BUNT. sound. **Most of his biggest current tracks are built on vocals sampled from existing songs**, usually emotive singer-songwriter or pop records:
- Nate Traveller's "You" became "Clouds".
- Elley Duhé's "LOST MY MIND" became "Lost My Mind (Wake Up)".
- Sam Tompkins's "Lose It All" became "Lose It All (I Don't Wanna)".
- Tom Odell's "Best Day of My Life" became the "BUNT. Version".
- TSHA feat. Nimmo's "OnlyL" became "Love Tonight".

The vocals are re-pitched and re-timed, single syllables are stretched into long held vowels, and the result is distorted and rhythmically chopped. **The processed vocal is the main hook.**

### Inferences
- **The vocal carries the main hook** on essentially every current hit. In several cases it is not a topline written for the track but a borrowed phrase from someone else's recording, reshaped by stretching, pitching, distortion and chopping. That is the single biggest reason a MIDI-only "folk house" model sounds nothing like BUNT.
- **How BUNT.'s "stutter" probably works**, combining Grimy Goods, Hypebot and the genre guides:
  - Take a short emotive phrase (2–4 words).
  - Time-stretch single vowels into sustained tones lasting a beat to a bar or more ("eeeeeea", "syyyyyy").
  - Apply a tempo-synced amplitude gate or tremolo (16th or 32nd notes, possibly varied) so the sustained vowel pulses ("stutters").
  - Add saturation or distortion.
  - Optionally re-pitch individual slices to follow the chord loop.

  This is inference. No source documents the exact gate rate or plugin chain for a specific BUNT. track.
- **Source-material taste:** singer-songwriter ballads or emotive pop (Tom Odell, Sam Tompkins, Nate Traveller, Elley Duhé), house vocals (TSHA/Nimmo), and Latin classics (Buena Vista Social Club). The "BUNT. Version" pattern is: take a slow ballad (Odell at 118 BPM) and re-contextualise it at 130 BPM.
- WhoSampled puts the vocal sample at 0:06 in "Lose It All", so the hook vocal enters almost immediately (Section 9).

### Gaps
- Exact gate/tremolo rates, whether the stutter is grid-locked or free-running, formant settings, and whether vocals were re-recorded by the featured artists after the sample flip. The "feat." credits on Clouds, Lost My Mind and Best Day suggest cleared samples or re-records, but this is unconfirmed.

## 13. What a generator can program as MIDI vs what depends on vocals, samples and sound design

### Takeaway
The **harmonic and rhythmic skeleton is easy to program**: short Aeolian loops, 127–140 BPM 4/4 (or 175 DnB), a sparse arrangement with 75–95-bar radio edits. The **identity of the current BUNT. sound lives in non-MIDI material**: borrowed, re-pitched, time-stretched, distorted and rhythmically gated vocal phrases that carry the hook. A generator that outputs only MIDI with generic instruments will not sound like BUNT. unless it includes a vocal-chop/stretch/gate engine, or exports explicit instructions or automation for one.

### Cited Findings
- His biggest current tracks are built on sampled vocals from existing records. — [WhoSampled (Lost My Mind)](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1048478/BUNT.-Elley-Duh%C3%A9-Lost-My-Mind-(Wake-Up)-Elley-Duh%C3%A9-LOST-MY-MIND/); [WhoSampled (Lose It All)](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1073995/BUNT.-Lose-It-All-(I-Don't-Wanna)-Sam-Tompkins-Lose-It-All/); [WhoSampled (Best Day)](https://www.whosampled.com/BUNT./Best-Day-of-My-Life-(BUNT.-Version)/); [WhoSampled (Love Tonight)](https://www.whosampled.com/BUNT./Love-Tonight/); [Grimy Goods (Clouds)](https://www.grimygoods.com/2023/01/17/artists-you-need-to-know-bunt-new-track-clouds-feat-nate-traveller/)
- Vowels are stretched into long held parts ("Clouds"), and the vocals are distorted. — [Grimy Goods](https://www.grimygoods.com/2023/01/17/artists-you-need-to-know-bunt-new-track-clouds-feat-nate-traveller/)
- Stutter house rests on a pulsating or stuttering vocal, lead or pad. — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2023/04/soundcloud-stutter-house-and-the-power-of-music-micro-genres.html)
- Commercial remakes need presets **and samples** in addition to MIDI to recreate the tracks. — [TopMusicArts stutter-house collection](https://topmusicarts.com/collections/new-ableton-templates/stutter-house)

### Inferences
| Element | MIDI-programmable? | Notes and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo / key / mode | **Yes** | 127–133 default, 140 secondary, 174–175 DnB; mostly minor for house mode (Section 2) |
| Chord loop | **Yes** | VI–i; VI–VII–i–VII; VImaj7–VII–i–VII; VI–iv–i; major mode ii–IV–I–I / ii–IV–I–V6. Start off the tonic; one loop per song (Section 3) |
| Keys/piano chord part ("gleaming keys") | **Yes** (notes); timbre needs a bright piano/EP sample | Grimy Goods; the Crown remake uses a grand piano |
| Kick/clap/hat | **Yes**, but BUNT.-specific patterns unverified | 4/4 house default; garage variant (Lost My Mind); DnB break variant (TRIPPIN) |
| DnB breakbeat | **Partly**: MIDI drum hits work, but authentic DnB usually uses sampled breaks | Inference |
| Sidechain pump | **Emulatable** with CC/volume automation | Remakes require Kickstart |
| Bass | **Yes** (root-following default), with no verified pattern | Section 5 gap |
| Stutter/tremolo rhythm | **Rhythm yes** (gate or retrigger patterns, 1/16 or 1/32, as MIDI or automation), **but it needs a sustained audio source** (a vocal vowel or pad) | Hypebot; Grimy Goods |
| Lead vocal hook | **No** | Borrowed or featured vocal phrase, re-pitched and re-timed independently (Section 2 inference), stretched vowels, distortion. Needs a sample/TTS/vocal-synth engine plus a chop/stretch/gate DSP chain |
| Vocal timbre (formant, saturation, delay throws) | **No** (DSP) | Genre guide: formant shifts, 3–7-semitone jumps, delay throws (unverified attribution) |
| Mix/master (loudness, possible off-440 tuning) | **No** | Clouds' ~450 Hz reading is unverified |
| Early-era folk leads (banjo/fiddle/trumpet/harmonica) | **Yes** (MIDI melody); realism depends on samples | Only for an "early BUNT." preset |

- **Rules that follow from the data:**
  1. Put the hook in the vocal layer, not in a synth riff.
  2. Keep harmony to one 2–4-chord loop, starting on VI in minor or ii in major.
  3. Prefer minor keys at 128–133 BPM; offer 140 and 175 modes.
  4. Keep the arrangement sparse ("bigger when there's less").
  5. Bring the hook in within the first bars.
  6. Target 2:10–3:05 for radio edits.
  7. Avoid I–V–vi–IV, organ stabs and harmonica/strummed-guitar leads in the current-era preset; no source supports them.
