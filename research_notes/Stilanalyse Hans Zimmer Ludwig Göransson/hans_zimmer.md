## 1. Harmony: typical progressions, harmonic devices, and key/tempo per cue

### Takeaway
The harmony that is documented is mostly minor-mode, triadic, loop-based and slow-moving. Four loops are attested best:
- **i–v–♭VII–IV**: "Time", A minor, about 60 BPM.
- **i–♭VI oscillation**: Dark Knight trilogy, D minor.
- **i–iiø7–i6–iv, rising stepwise in D minor**: the 1990s–2000s "Zimmer progression".
- **i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII**: forum-attributed, under 16th-note spiccato ostinatos.

Most signature cues are listed in A minor or D minor, or in their relative majors (C or F) in sheet-music listings. Hard data per cue is thin and often contradicts itself. "The Battle", "A Dark Knight", "Supermarine", "Paul's Dream" and "Ripples in the Sand" have no sourced key or BPM at all.

### Inferences
- The Musicnotes suffixes "_U2" and "_D2" look like transposed versions of the same product. The B-minor "Time" listing is therefore probably a transposition of the A-minor original edition. This is inferred from the URL pattern.
- **Reading "Time" in Roman numerals, with A minor as tonic**:
  - Am–Em–G–D = **i–v–♭VII–IV**, and Am–Cmaj7–G–D = **i–♭IIImaj7–♭VII–IV**.
  - The major IV (D major, which contains F♯) borrows the raised Dorian 6th in an otherwise Aeolian loop.
  - The minor v, rather than a major V, avoids a dominant cadence. The loop never resolves functionally.
- **The stepwise "Zimmer progression" spelled out in D minor**: Dm – Em7♭5 – Dm/F – Gm. The bass rises D–E–F–G (scale steps 1–2–3–4) under largely held upper voices. The loop ends on iv, so a minor-plagal iv→i return is built into it. This spelling is my inference from the Roman numerals in the VI-Control snippet.
- **Relative-major ambiguity**: several cues are listed in both a minor key and its relative major (Cornfield Chase in A minor and C major; First Step in A minor and C major). The vi–IV–I–V dissent points the same way. These look like Aeolian loops that can be heard either way.
  - Generator suggestion: store progressions relative to the minor tonic, and offer the label "minor / relative major" as a display choice only.
- **Harmonic rhythm**: suppose "Time"'s four-chord loop fills the 32-beat repeating unit Shaun Duke describes (section 5). Then each chord lasts 8 beats, which is 2 bars of 4/4, or 8 s at 60 BPM.
  - Generator default: one chord per 2 bars for slow cues (≤70 BPM), one chord per bar for driving cues (95–130 BPM).
- **Default keys**: A minor and D minor, the most documented, with C major and F major as relative majors. A major-mode preset would suit the pastoral 1990s material: "This Land" appears in C major and "Now We Are Free" in A major in arrangements.
- **"Tension/drone" preset**: in "Why So Serious?" the harmony shrinks to one or two sustained pitches a whole step apart, centred on D. A preset could alternate the tonic with the whole step below (i ↔ ♭VII dyad), or hold a single pitch with a slow upward bend or cluster swell, with no chord changes.
- **Devices without direct sources**: sus2/sus4 voicings, chromatic mediants, and modal mixture beyond the borrowed IV-in-minor are plausible, but no reachable source quantified them. Greig's neo-Riemannian keyword suggests that chromatic third relations matter analytically at least for Crimson Tide (1995). Treat these as optional "colour" switches, not defaults.

## 2. Melody: motif length, intervals, rhythm, repetition, range

### Takeaway
Zimmer's signature themes from the Nolan era onward are deliberately minimal. They are built from two-note cells:
- Batman: a rising minor third.
- The Joker: two sustained pitches a whole step apart.
- Superman: a two-note figure, according to one blog.

Note values are long ("whole-note themes"), and themes are repeated and re-orchestrated rather than developed. Zimmer himself credits simplicity as his tool for keeping a theme. The 1994–2000 scores were more lyrical and theme-rich (19 themes were sketched for Gladiator), with melodies carried by solo voices and solo instruments.

### Inferences
- **Motif length**: 2 notes (Batman, Joker, and Superman per the blog) up to a 4-chord, multi-bar cell ("Time"). Generator lead default:
  - a 2–4-note motif
  - long values (whole and half notes at 60–100 BPM)
  - repeated once per loop and re-orchestrated or re-registered, not varied
- **Intervals**: the rising minor third ("heroic/ominous" cell), the major second ("menace/drone" cell) and stepwise neighbour motion are documented. Perfect 4ths and 5ths for brass calls are plausible but unsourced.
- **Range**: given 2-note cells, a motif's span is probably at most a 5th and a whole theme's at most an octave. No source measured this, so it is an inference.
- **Repetition**: in an additive build at 60 BPM with a 32-beat loop, a 4.5-minute cue holds about 8 repetitions (4.5 × 60 = 270 beats ÷ 32 ≈ 8.4). This is arithmetic, not a sourced count.
- **Era difference**:
  - 1994–2000 (Lion King, Gladiator): longer, voice-led lyrical lines and more themes.
  - 2005–2014: cell-based, with the "melody" often doubled by low brass in whole notes.
  - Dune: voice-like lines, chant or heartbeat vocals, ornamented solo winds such as the duduk.
- **Lead rhythm for the generator**: mostly whole and half notes, entering on the downbeat or as a short anacrusis; in the 1990s variant, allow dotted rhythms and longer phrases. Inference, not sourced.

## 3. Ostinatos and rhythm: string ostinatos, keyboard figures, percussion, ticking clocks

### Takeaway
Repeating figures, not melody, carry the propulsion:
- **Strings**: 16th-note spiccato in broken triads or oscillating minor thirds (Batman Begins, Inception openings).
- **Keyboards**: repetitive organ and piano figures (Interstellar).
- **Clocks**: a tick every 1.25 s in Interstellar's "Mountains" (fan-reported). Dunkirk is built from Nolan's pocket watch turned into a synth sound, a "double-time pulse".
- **Drum ensembles**: three-taiko unison ensembles (Spitfire HZ Percussion), a 12-drummer circle for Man of Steel, and a low tom plus bass drum pattern under "Time".

In Dune, all percussion was synthesised, and a vocal "heartbeat" supplies the pulse.

### Cited Findings
- **String ostinatos**:
  - Batman Begins: the two-note theme sits over "a systematic ostinato rhythm played by real and electric strings" — [Filmtracks](https://filmtracks.com/titles/batman_begins.html)
  - the rising minor third "often appears as a rapidly oscillating ostinato in the strings" (citing Halfyard) — [Scribd analysis](https://www.scribd.com/document/558881591/dark-knight-analysis)
  - spiccato 16ths moving in a broken triad, usually i–VI–III–VII (openings of Batman and Inception) — [VSL Forum (user)](https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/200198)
  - forum users explain that spiccato lets the bow bounce so the string rings briefly, and that it suits fast repeated figures better than full staccato, which "could easily sound too overbearing and too stiff" — [VSL Forum](https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/230623)
  - Zimmer on the Batman scores (MasterClass): "these ostinato patterns, they were all done separately, which seemed like a really good idea at the time" — [MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/hans-zimmer-teaches-film-scoring/chapters/character-theme-batman)
  - Rick Beato's Dark Knight video focuses on its recurring ostinato patterns and argues the concept can be applied to any chord or scale — [Rosetta / Rick Beato](https://rosetta.to/u/rickbeato/hans-zimmer-james-newton-howard-music-of-the-dark-knight)
  - a retailer blog says Zimmer uses string ostinatos "to create tension or a heartbeat-like pulse" — [Bax-shop blog (low authority)](https://www.bax-shop.co.uk/blog/studio-recording/how-to-compose-for-film-like-hans-zimmer/)
- **Keyboards**: "No Time for Caution" is described as a repetitive organ motif over pulsating strings, with intensity rising steadily. — [oembed.org blog (low reliability)](https://oembed.org/blog/interstellar-soundtrack-the-no-time)

### Inferences
- **Tick arithmetic**: a tick every 1.25 s is 48 BPM (60 / 1.25). If each tick were exactly one Earth day, one hour on Miller's planet would be 2,880 ticks, against about 2,557 days in seven years. The tick-as-day mapping is therefore only approximate (about 8 years per hour); treat it as fan lore.
- **Ostinato lane**:
  - 16th notes (4 per beat) on chord tones: a broken triad (root–5th–octave–3rd or similar) or a root and minor-third oscillation
  - re-voiced on each chord of the progression
  - cello/viola register
  - gate about 40–60 % of the step for a spiccato feel; accent on beat starts
  - an organ/piano arpeggio option in 8ths or 16ths for "Interstellar" mode
- **Accent groupings**: 3+3+2 inside 8 sixteenths is often associated with this style, but no source supported it. Offer it as an optional switch, not a default.
- **Drum-lane archetypes**:
  - (a) **"Time" bed**: low tom plus kick on strong beats, entering after the first loops.
  - (b) **Ensemble build**: taiko/tom unison hits on downbeats, rolls into section changes, gong or low boom at the climax.
  - (c) **Clock**: a constant tick on quarters (48–60 BPM) or 8ths (the "double-time pulse"), high-pitched and dry.
  - (d) **Drum circle**: a dense multi-player tom/snare groove for the heroic climax (Man of Steel).
  - (e) **Synthetic**: processed hits and a heartbeat pulse (Dune).
- **Clock-locked tempo**: Dunkirk locks the music to an external mechanical pulse (watch, engine). A generator could lock all lanes to one tick track and raise intensity by adding subdivisions and layers rather than raising the BPM, or add a Shepard-style riser (section 4).

### Gaps
- No sourced BPM for the Dunkirk tick and no tempo map for "Supermarine".
- No transcription-level data on the ostinato rhythms of "Molossus", "Time" or Interstellar (only forum descriptions), and no accent-pattern data.
- No source describes taiko patterns in specific cues; no meter information for "The Battle".

## 4. Orchestration and sound: instrument roles, registers, BRAAAM, Shepard tone, hybrid production, libraries, interviews

### Takeaway
Zimmer's sound is a large, bottom-heavy hybrid orchestra:
- massed low brass: Inception's "BRAAAM" used 12 trombones, 6 horns and 4 tubas, 22 brass players in total
- cello-heavy strings: the HZ Strings library records 60 violins and 60 cellos
- organ (Interstellar's 1926 Harrison & Harrison at Temple Church) and piano
- electronics treated as equal partners
- electric guitar (Johnny Marr's 12-string)
- large drum ensembles
- choirs and solo voices: Lebo M, Lisa Gerrard, London Voices, Loire Cotler

Psychoacoustic devices (the Shepard tone, ticking) are part of the composition. With Dune, the palette moved to invented instruments, processed female voices, bagpipes and synthesised percussion.

### Inferences
- **Consistency check**: Variety's 12 trombones + 6 horns + 4 tubas = 22, which matches Wikipedia's "22 brass players". The BRAAAM is therefore a low-brass tutti of about 22 players with free pitch "within a certain range", effectively a dense low cluster or power chord.
- **Bottom-heavy strings**: HZ Strings records as many cellos as violins (60/60), and the Crimson Tide choir sits in its lowest male register. Zimmer's ensemble balance leans deliberately low.
- **Register suggestions** (MIDI note numbers with 60 = middle C; FL Studio labels MIDI 60 as "C5" by default, which is general knowledge, not researched here):
  - BRAAAM / low brass: root, 5th and octave at about MIDI 28–52 (E1–E3); 1–2-bar sustains with a swell; trigger at section starts.
  - horn/brass theme: about MIDI 50–72.
  - string ostinato: about MIDI 48–72 (cello/viola).
  - sustained chord pad (strings or organ): about MIDI 55–84.
  - bass: octave root pedal at about MIDI 24–43 (synth sub plus contrabass/tuba).
  - piano: sparse mid register, about MIDI 57–81.
  - low male choir: about MIDI 38–62.
  - organ: pedal tone at about MIDI 24–36 plus manual figures above.
- **Mapping the generator's five lanes to sourced roles**:
  - chords: whole-note sustained strings/brass, or organ in "Interstellar" mode.
  - bass: root pedal in octaves (synth plus low strings/tuba).
  - lead: horns/low brass (Nolan); solo female voice, duduk or solo cello (Gladiator/Dune); piano ("Time", Interstellar); electric guitar (Marr, "Flight").
  - counter-line/ostinato: 16th spiccato strings, organ/piano arpeggio, or a clock tick.
  - drums: taiko/tom ensemble or synthesised hits.
- **Shepard-tone riser as a MIDI device**: the same rising scale or chromatic line doubled in 3–4 octaves, with velocity fading in on the lowest octave and out on the highest, looped. This approximates the documented stacked-octave construction.

## 5. Form, cue lengths, eras, and suggested generator variants

### Takeaway
Zimmer cues characteristically build by addition. "Time" is the documented model: a short repeating loop (a 32-beat unit) that starts with piano plus one or two electronic tones, adds one to four elements on each pass, peaks, then falls back to the progression on solo piano.

Album cues are long in the Nolan and Dune periods, about 5–9+ minutes: "Ripples in the Sand" 5:14, "Paul's Dream" 7:03, "Supermarine" 8:03, "Why So Serious?" 9:14. The 1994 Lion King score piece "This Land" is about 3 minutes.

Three periods stand out:
- **1994–2003**: the "power anthem" plus vocal and world-music colours.
- **2005–2014**: the Nolan "minimal theme, maximal sound" period, with two-note motifs, ostinatos, low brass, organ and ticking.
- **2017–2024**: sound-design-led scores (Shepard tone and watch tick in Dunkirk; invented instruments, synthesised percussion and voices in Dune).

### Inferences

#### Generic additive-build template
This template is inferred from the "Time" accounts. It assumes an 8-bar (32-beat) loop at 60 BPM, which is 32 s per loop:
- **Loop 1**: piano chords plus a soft pad or electronic tone.
- **Loop 2**: add the octave bass pedal and low sustained strings.
- **Loop 3**: add the 16th ostinato (strings, or organ/piano).
- **Loop 4**: add low tom plus kick; brass enters with the whole-note motif.
- **Loops 5–6**: full tutti with BRAAAM-type low-brass swells, choir or organ, ensemble drums; peak.
- **Loop 7 or last**: release by stripping back to solo piano.

This gives about 3.5–4.5 minutes. For a 5–9-minute "suite", add a second, faster section or repeat the build.

#### Suggested generator variants (inference; numbers are taken from the data cited above where available)
