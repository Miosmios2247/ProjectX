## 1. Black Panther (2018) and Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022): talking drum, fula flute, Baaba Maal, how African percussion, orchestra and trap are combined; cue data

### Takeaway
Black Panther is built from field research in Senegal (with Baaba Maal's circle) layered onto a large Western orchestra and choir. T'Challa's identity is a **talking-drum "speech rhythm" of his name**, doubled by a **Roland TR-808** "to beef it up" and crowned by **royal brass**. Killmonger's identity is a **Senegalese fula flute** (Amadou Ba) played around a single root note, with screams and speech blown into the instrument, later **distorted** as he gains power, over **trap-style beats**. Hard numbers are scarce. Automatic readings: "Wakanda" about **117 BPM, C major**; "Killmonger's Dream" about **128 BPM, G minor**. Both are low-confidence. Wakanda Forever replaces the Senegalese palette with reconstructed Mayan/Mesoamerican instruments (conch, ancient flutes) and Nigerian musicians, and is more vocal-driven.

### Inferences
- **The speech-rhythm motif is the core device (inference).** Both character identities come from spoken words turned into musical rhythm: the drum "speaks" T'Challa's name, and Ba screams "Killmonger" into the flute. A generator could derive the percussion or counter-line motif from the syllables of a word: one hit per syllable, accent on the stressed syllable, 2–3 pitches with glides. (The pitch glide is general talking-drum organology, not from a source here.)
- **Layering rule (inference from the 808 "beef it up" facts):** the 808 kick/sub should *double the accents of the hand-drum pattern*, not play a generic four-on-the-floor or boom-bap pattern. The acoustic part carries the identity; the electronic part adds weight.
- **Killmonger formula (inference):** a lead that hovers around the tonic ("revolved around a root note") with breath/scream articulations and growing distortion, over trap drums. In MIDI: tonic-heavy melody, grace notes, pitch-bend scoops, velocity spikes. Trap kit: 1/16 hats with 1/32 rolls and a half-time snare. The kit details come from genre convention plus the "trap beat" descriptions, not from a Göransson statement.
- **Tempo/key defaults (inference, low confidence):** about 115–130 BPM (from the 117 and 128 automatic readings). Minor key, G minor as one plausible default, for the Killmonger/dark mode. Major (C major reading) for the "Wakanda" reveal. Motif length about 6 notes for the royal brass theme.
- **Distortion as a dramatic dial (inference):** the Decapitator usage implies a "power/intensity" parameter. In FL Studio, raise saturation (e.g., Fruity Blood Overdrive or Fruity Soft Clipper) and velocity on the lead as the cue intensifies.
- **Wakanda Forever variant (inference):** swap talking drums for udu/clay and conch/ancient-flute colours, and give vocal lines more weight.

## 2. The Mandalorian (2019–): bass recorder lead, synths, drum machine/percussion, orchestra; tempo, key, harmony

### Takeaway
The theme grew from Göransson playing a **bass recorder** himself, after deliberately stepping away from the computer. He processed it with **delay and reverb** and built around it a **heartbeat-like drum, a bassline, piano, acoustic and electric guitar, a 1970s Roland GR-500 guitar synthesizer, other synths and "metal sounds"**, then a **70-piece (also reported as 75) orchestra**. The official piano edition gives **C minor, ♩ = 75, 4/4**. A Point Blank analysis ties the theme to the **half-whole diminished (octatonic) scale** and says it creates "rising tension". Morricone's westerns are among the stated influences.

### Cited Findings
- He stepped away from the computer and returned to a childhood instrument, the recorder. He bought a set of different recorders. In his words: "Just sitting down, mediating [sic], just closing my eyes, and just recording myself playing the bass recorder for three days." — [KCRW](https://www.kcrw.com/shows/press-play-with-madeleine-brand/stories/ludwig-goransson-mandalorian-score-star-wars) [SS]
- "That feeling was to step away from the computer and use real instruments, instruments I could touch." The recorder riff was paired with a **heartbeat sound and a bassline** — [Sonos interview](https://www.sonos.com/en-gb/blog/mandalorian-composer-interview) [SS; page fetch blocked]
- He shut himself in his studio for about a month with instruments and no computers — [Monsters and Critics (Disney Gallery ep. 7 review)](https://monstersandcritics.com/tv/disney-gallery-star-wars-the-mandalorian-episode-7-review-score) [SS]. This conflicts with the "three days" in KCRW; the two may describe different phases.
- He spent "like 10 hours in one day just playing different melodies on the recorder". He recorded **set sounds** such as the clink of the Mandalorian's boot spurs and worked them into the music — [Insider via Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.com/mandalorian-composer-says-theres-hidden-192151865.html) [SS]
- The recorder was processed with "some modern production, like the delay and reverb" — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2019/12/06/the-mandalorian-composer-ludwig-goransson-interview-disney-plus-star-wars) [SS; page fetch blocked]
- Göransson worried the idea was too simple. Favreau reacted after the first few seconds of the flute sound that this was the theme — [Variety (video)](https://variety.com/video/mandalorian-theme-instrument-soundstrack-star-wars) [SS]
- The score began with a cheap (£10) recorder and an acoustic guitar — [Fan Tha Tracks](https://www.fanthatracks.com/?p=83464) [SS]
- **Layering order:** he began with the recorder, then added **piano, guitar and drums**. He used a **Roland GR-500**, a 1970s guitar synthesizer played through an electric guitar — [Laughing Place, Disney Gallery ep. 7 recap](https://www.laughingplace.com/w/articles/2020/06/12/what-we-learned-from-disney-gallery-the-mandalorian-episode-7-score/) [SS]. The drums gave the theme a **heartbeat** feel — [Laughing Place, *For Scores* podcast recap](https://www.laughingplace.com/w/articles/2021/02/27/for-scores-ludwig-goransson-star-wars-mandalorian-recap/) [SS]
- He played many key instruments himself (unusual woodwinds, drums, guitars, piano, percussion) and added a **70-piece orchestra**. He "combined his recordings of individual acoustic instruments with more modern sounds, including synthesizers and cutting-edge processing". Because the Mandalorian never removes his helmet, he added **"metal sounds"** — [Variety, Nov 2019](https://variety.com/2019/tv/production/star-wars-mandalorian-music-1203402950) [SS]
- Orchestra size conflicts: Disney says "a full 75-piece orchestra" — [The Walt Disney Company](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/?p=18355) [SS]. Season 2 used a masked, socially distanced **40-piece string orchestra** — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/mandalorian-ludwig-goransson-score-distinctly-different-original-star-war-music/) [SS]
- Ennio Morricone's score for *The Humanoid* helped him "think about Star Wars music in a new way" — [Inverse](https://inverse.com/entertainment/mandalorian-composer-ludwig-goransson-interview) [SS]
- **Harmony:** Point Blank lecturer Claude Ruelle analyses "the theme's **Half-Whole Diminished Scale** and shows how it creates a rising tension". The recreation uses Spitfire Hans Zimmer Percussion and Abbey Road One — [DJ Mag / Point Blank Deconstruction](https://djmag.com/pointblank/point-blank-deconstruction-mandalorian-theme-song-ludwig-g-ransson-w-claude-ruelle); [Point Blank blog](https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/blog/deconstruction-the-mandalorian-theme-song-by-ludwig-goransson-w-claude-ruelle/) [SS]
- **Key and tempo (published arrangements):** the official piano solo (Walt Disney Music Publishing, 2019) is listed in **C minor, ♩ = 75, 4/4** — [Sheet Music Direct](https://www.sheetmusicdirect.com/en-US/se/ID_No/437132/Product.aspx) [SS; listing attribution per summary]. One Musicnotes arrangement lists **D minor**. Other arrangement tempo markings range from about **75 to 152 BPM**, including "quickly" at about 152 — [Musicnotes](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/betacustic/star-wars-the-mandalorian-main-theme-beginner/MN0257581); [MakeMusic (Longfield arr.)](https://shop.makemusic.com/products/the-mandalorian-from-star-wars-the-mandalorian-arr-robert-longfield-35-pk00486489) [SS]

### Inferences
- **Tempo (inference):** ♩ = 75 and about 152 are a half/double pair. The theme is best modeled as a slow harmonic pulse (about 72–80 BPM) over percussion that often moves on a 16th grid (felt as about 150).
- **Harmony (inference, derived only from the half-whole-diminished claim; this is not a transcription):** on a C tonic the half-whole set is C–D♭–E♭–E–F♯–G–A–B♭. It contains major and minor triads on **C, E♭, F♯/G♭ and A**, a cycle of minor thirds. Generator options:
  - a minor tonic pedal (i) with a **♭II neighbour** (D♭) for tension;
  - progressions such as **i – ♭III – ♭V(♯IV) – i** or **i – ♭III – VI(♮) – i**;
  - a diminished-7th chord on the tonic (i°7) as a passing chord;
  - for "western heroic" climaxes, a conventional **i – ♭VI – ♭VII – i**.
- **Lead register (inference; general organology, not sourced):** the bass recorder sounds roughly **F3–G5 (MIDI 53–79)**. Use long tones with short pickup figures, plenty of rest between phrases, and heavy dotted-1/8 delay plus long reverb for the "space flute" effect. In FL Studio: Fruity Delay 3 and Fruity Reeverb 2.
- **Drums (inference):** a "heartbeat" kick (two close hits, e.g., beat 1 plus the following 16th, repeated on beat 3), sparse hand drums and toms, and metallic clinks on off-beats in place of hi-hats. Snare rolls and cymbal swells lead into orchestral climaxes.
- **Build (directly from the Laughing Place layering order):** solo recorder, then heartbeat drums and bass, then piano and guitar, then synths, then full orchestra.

### Gaps
- No tempo or key data from the recording itself (no Tunebat/SongBPM page surfaced). No Roman-numeral analysis of the theme or of typical cues. The Point Blank video content is known only from the summary.
- Claims I could not pin down:
  - "the intro is the bass recorder with effects, so it kind of sounds almost like a **space flute**";
  - "every episode, we recorded with a 70-piece orchestra, with the LA Studio Symphony at Fox";
  - a "three-part approach" of organic instruments, synthesizers and symphonic orchestra;
  - **programmed drum sounds, African drums, jaw harp and cymbals dipped in water**;
  - Favreau's "samurai/western hybrid" brief with Morricone and Kurosawa as references;
  - heavy Morricone-style instrumentation in "Chapter 9: The Marshal".
- I did not find whether a classic drum machine model was used; only "programmed drums" is mentioned, and that claim is unpinned.
- Blocked hosts relevant here: sonos.com and fortune.com (full interviews).

## 3. Oppenheimer (2023): solo violin, fast string ostinatos, accelerating tempos, "Can You Hear the Music", "Fission", "Destroyer of Worlds"

### Takeaway
Nolan asked for the **violin** to embody Oppenheimer. Göransson's wife, violinist **Serena (McKinney) Göransson**, recorded the solo lines. They exploit the violin's ability to move from romantic vibrato to "neurotic and horrific" glissandi. The ensemble is about **70 players, centred on strings plus synthesizers**, with brass in support, few woodwinds and **no drums**. "Can You Hear the Music" was built from a **4-note bassline**, then a **6-note ("hexatonic") violin melody**, then a **counter-melody**. It has **21 tempo changes in about 2 minutes**, reportedly starting around **80 BPM and ending about three times as fast**, and was recorded live to a click track that changed tempo. Published arrangements are mostly in **G major** (one in E minor), one of them in **3/4**.

### Cited Findings
- "For that sequence, Göransson wrote a **two-minute piece with 21 tempo changes**." He expected to "do it in segments and record it bar by bar". His wife Serena, "also an accomplished violinist", made a suggestion. The musicians ultimately played to a **click track with multiple tempo changes** — [The Hollywood Reporter via Yahoo, "Recorded the Explosive Score in Five Days"](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/oppenheimer-composer-ludwig-g-ransson-155559533.html); [Slashfilm](https://slashfilm.com/1345110/ludwig-goransson-oppenheimer-score-five-days) [SS]
- 21 tempo changes, "a large feat for a live orchestra" — [Wikipedia: Oppenheimer (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(soundtrack)) [SS]
- "It starts at **80 bpm** and it ends **three times as fast**" — [A Rabbit's Foot](https://a-rabbitsfoot.com/?p=10789) [SS]
- A fan-style analysis says the cue creates continuous acceleration by "adding **70bpm** at every shift from ascending to descending" — [Tonebase violin blog](https://tonebase.co/violin-blog-posts/ludwig-goransson-oppenheimers-violin-infused-soundtrack-wins-oscar-for-best-score) [SS; page fetch blocked]. **This conflicts arithmetically** with 80 → 240 BPM over 21 changes (see Inferences).
- He reportedly called it "unplayable", and the orchestra spent **three days** on it before pulling it off — [Audio Network](https://blog.audionetwork.com/the-edit/music/oppenheimer-soundtrack-review) [SS]
- **How the cue was built:** he wrote a **bassline of four notes first**, then a **six-note melody** on top, which Serena recorded on violin, then added a **counter-melody**. The idea took shape after he saw IMAX VFX tests of "molecules swimming around". He asked: "How the hell are we gonna be able to record this live?" — [MusicRadar (drawing on Variety)](https://www.musicradar.com/news/oppenheimer-ludwig-goransson-synth-can-you-hear-the-music) [SS]
- "I had this violin line which was a **hexatonic, it's a six-note scale**, and I started playing around with it and taking the sound up and down, and **every time it changed direction I would do a tempo change**." — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/oppenheimer-ludwig-goransson-synth-can-you-hear-the-music) [SS, attribution by context; the original is probably the Variety interview]
- "The violin, depending on how you perform it, you can go from being the most beautiful, romantic tone, and within a split second change to something **neurotic and horrific**." Nolan's rationale: "there's a tension to the sound in a way that I think fits the highly-strung intellect and emotion of Robert Oppenheimer." — [Wikipedia: Oppenheimer (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(soundtrack)) [SS, attribution by wording; original interviews not identified]
- **Ensemble:** "a 70-member orchestra" whose sound centres on the **interplay between strings and synthesizers**, with brass in a supporting role, "largely devoid of woodwinds and prominent percussion" — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/oppenheimer.html) [SS]
- He added synthesizers so the score would sound timeless despite the 1940s setting. He and Nolan **skipped drums**. The score was recorded at the **Eastwood Scoring Stage, Burbank** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(soundtrack)) [SS; snippets derived from Wikipedia]
- The score was recorded in five days (headline) — [THR via Yahoo](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/oppenheimer-composer-ludwig-g-ransson-155559533.html). Audio Network's "three days" for one cue is not necessarily a contradiction.
- **Published keys of "Can You Hear the Music" arrangements:** most Musicnotes arrangements are in **G major** (e.g., Laurie Théberge, "Original Published Key: G Major", **in 3/4**; also Patrik Pietschmann, nattaliepiano, Mark Fowler) — [Musicnotes Oppenheimer page](https://www.musicnotes.com/shows/oppenheimer) [SS]. One piano arrangement (Willian Ciriaco) is labelled **E minor**, "original key" by the arranger — [Sheet Music Plus/Musicroom listing](https://blog.musicroom.com/en/product/can-you-hear-the-music-22700204.html) [SS; exact listing not pinned]

- **Generator implementation (inference):** FL Studio supports tempo automation, but a non-musician-friendly option is **rhythmic diminution at a fixed tempo**: ♩ → ♪ → triplet ♪ → 𝅘𝅥𝅯 → 𝅘𝅥𝅯 triplets. Trigger each density step when the ostinato line changes direction, which is Göransson's own stated rule.
- **Pitch set (inference):** if the unpinned "B minor + C major triad" claim is right, the six notes are **B C D E F♯ G**, a G-major/E-minor scale with A omitted. That matches the G major / E minor arrangement keys. Two-chord oscillation **v – ♭VI in E minor (Bm – C), i.e. iii – IV in G major**, would be a natural generator choice. Alternatives: **i – ♭VI – ♭III – ♭VII** (Em – C – G – D) or a tonic pedal with ♭VI alternation.
- **Ostinato shapes (inference):** scalar or broken-chord **16th-note runs** confined to the six-note set, rising then falling. The bass is a **4-note cell** in half/whole notes. The counter-melody enters only after the main line is established.
- **Lead behaviour (inference):** a "romantic ↔ neurotic" macro. One end is legato long notes with vibrato (mod wheel); the other is fast spiccato or tremolo with **wide glissandi** (pitch bend of ±12 semitones or more). Violin register **G3–E6 (MIDI 55–88)** (organology, not sourced).
- **Drum role (inference from "skipped drums"):** off by default. At most, low synth sub-booms or single timpani-like hits at phrase peaks. Brass or brass-synth swells mark the build. End sections with abrupt silence.

## 4. Tenet (2020): synth bass, reversed sounds, heavy electronic rhythm, Travis Scott ("The Plan"); tempos, keys

### Takeaway
Tenet's sound comes from material that is **reversed, processed and stretched until it is neither clearly orchestral nor clearly electronic**. Percussion rhythms were reversed, re-performed by players imitating the reversed audio, and reversed again. Themes were played with vibrato so the reversed audio would be effective. Synths were played in the room with the strings. Heartbeats and **Nolan's own heavy breathing** (Sator's sound) are woven into racing drum-driven cues. Travis Scott's "The Plan" was written over the beat of the score cue **"Trucks in Place"** and produced by Göransson with WondaGurl. **I found no reliable BPM or key for any Tenet track.**

### Cited Findings

Mixgraph lists the code **"8012A"** for "The Plan – From the Motion Picture TENET" — [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/artists/travis-scott) [SS; meaning unclear]

### Inferences
- **Reading the Mixgraph code (inference, unverified):** "8012A" may mean **80 BPM** plus Camelot **12A = D♭/C♯ minor**. If so, 80 BPM in trap could equally be felt as 160. Treat this as a hypothesis only.
- **Generator "Inversion" behaviour (inference):**
  - (a) Write **palindromic or retrograde-friendly motifs** (motif followed by its reverse, or symmetric rhythms) so they make sense backwards.
  - (b) Use **reverse-swell envelopes** in MIDI: velocities ramp up to an abrupt cut. In FL Studio, reverse audio or use long attack with fast release.
  - (c) Add a **driving 16th-note synth-bass pulse on a tonic pedal** under "racing" drums.
  - (d) Layer breath and heartbeat samples.
- **Drum role (inference):** percussion is the engine, not decoration: fast, propulsive patterns, some reversed. Without data, a working range of **120–140 BPM**, or a half-time trap feel for "The Plan"-type material, is a design choice, not a measured value.
- **Score-to-song pipeline (inference):** the "Trucks in Place" → "The Plan" path shows that a Göransson cue is often a **beat-plus-texture loop** a rapper can write over. This supports making the drum/bass loop the generator's primary layer.

## 5. Creed (2015) and the Childish Gambino productions ("Redbone", "This Is America") as they shape his film style

### Takeaway
Creed established the template: a **large orchestra (reported 100 pieces) plus choir over hip-hop-style beats built from boxing-gym sounds used as drums**, mixing "classical and jazz to rap and hip-hop". Tunebat reads "Conlan Fight" at **G minor, 130 BPM**. With Childish Gambino he produced "Redbone", a live-band 70s-funk ballad at about **80 BPM** whose published sheet music is in **E♭ minor**, and "This Is America" at about **120 BPM (or 60 half-time)**. The latter alternates an Afro/gospel section (triplet vocals over straight 16ths) with **trap drops** over a low, detuned bass that Ethan Hein hears as **E♭ Phrygian**. These are the same "organic ensemble versus 808/trap" contrasts that Black Panther and Tenet use.

- **Tempo bands seen in this corpus (inference from the readings above):** about **80 BPM** (Redbone; possibly The Plan) and about **120–130 BPM** (This Is America, Conlan Fight, Killmonger's Dream). Both work in a half-time feel.
- **Modal colour (inference):** Phrygian **i–♭II** oscillation in the bass (Hein's E♭–E reading) and minor keys (G minor, E♭ minor) recur. A generator "dark/trap" switch could force ♭2 in the bass line.
- **Section contrast (inference):** an organic or gospel/acoustic intro of about 16 bars, then a hard trap drop with a sub-bass takeover. This is a reusable arrangement macro, comparable to Black Panther's solo flute/voice → drums → tutti and The Mandalorian's solo recorder → beat → orchestra.
- **Polyrhythm (inference):** a counter-line in **1/8-note triplets** against **straight 16th-note** guitar/hat patterns is a cheap way to get the Afro-diasporic tension Hein describes.

## 6. Across works: harmony, melody, ostinatos, drums/percussion, orchestration, registers, production approach, cue building; Göransson on his process

### Takeaway
What makes Göransson recognisable is mostly **a method, not a fixed harmonic vocabulary**:
1. Research, often in the field, leads to **one signature, usually non-orchestral solo voice per project**: talking drum and fula flute; bass recorder; solo violin; reversed sounds and breath.
2. That voice is **recorded live**, by specialists or by Göransson himself, away from the computer, and then **processed**: delay and reverb, distortion, reversal, stretching.
3. It is **layered onto beat-driven production**: 808s, trap hats, heartbeat kicks, found-sound drums.
4. A **large orchestra and choir** comes in at the climaxes, and cues **build additively** from a solo motif to tutti.

The few hard data points lean towards **minor keys** (C minor, G minor, E♭ minor) and **modal or symmetric colour** (Phrygian ♭2, half-whole diminished, six-note "hexatonic" sets). Motifs are **short, rhythm-defined cells**: name rhythms, a 4-note bass under a 6-note melody, a 6-note brass motif.

### Inferences
- **Harmony (inference):** keep harmony **static and pedal-based** under strongly characterised solo lines: a tonic drone or bass pedal, with upper chords alternating between two or three neighbours. Recurring colours worth parameterising:
  - natural minor (Aeolian) as the default;
  - **Phrygian ♭II** for trap/villain colour;
  - **octatonic / minor-third-cycle** triads for sci-fi tension (Mandalorian);
  - **six-note "gapped" scales** for the Oppenheimer-style ostinato (omit one diatonic degree).
- **Example progressions for the generator (inference; none of these is a transcription of an actual cue):**
  - **i – ♭VI – ♭VII – i** (heroic minor, brass/choir climaxes);
  - **i – ♭II – i – ♭VII** (Phrygian trap);
  - **i – ♭III – ♭V(♯IV) – i** (octatonic tension);
  - **v – ♭VI** two-chord oscillation (Oppenheimer-style, if the B-minor + C-major claim holds);
  - **i (pedal) with ♭VI/♭VII upper-structure alternation** (drone sections).
- **Melody (inference):** motifs of **2–6 notes**, defined by rhythm first (speech rhythm, heartbeat, direction changes). They are repeated, re-orchestrated and intensified (processing, density, tempo) rather than spun out into long tunes. Prefer **tonic-centred lines** with ornaments and slides (fula flute, violin glissando, recorder bends).
- **Ostinato figures (inference):**
  - (a) syllable-rhythm drum cells (1–2 bars);
  - (b) 16th-note scalar or arpeggio runs that reverse direction (Oppenheimer);
  - (c) 8th-note plucked or synth pulses on root and fifth (Mandalorian);
  - (d) 808 sub notes locked to hand-drum accents (Black Panther).
- **Drum/percussion roles (inference): four distinct modes.**
  - **Carrier**: world percussion *is* the theme (talking drum).
  - **Weight**: 808 or trap kit doubling the accents.
  - **Pulse**: heartbeat kick, sparse (Mandalorian).
  - **Absent**: Oppenheimer, where rhythm comes from strings and tempo changes.
- **Registers (inference):**
  - lead in low-mid (bass recorder about F3–G5) or high (violin up to about E6, fula flute mid-high);
  - bass in the sub range (808, about C1–G2);
  - choir and brass in mid range (about C3–C5);
  - ostinato strings in mid-high.
- **Cue-build macro (inference, consistent across the cited builds):** solo signature voice, then a rhythmic layer (drums or ostinato), then bass weight (808 or synth), then harmonic bed (piano/guitar/synth), then orchestra and choir tutti, optionally followed by an abrupt cut to silence.

## 7. Suggested generator variants (each with tempo range, typical progressions, instruments and drum role)

### Inferences

**Variant A: "Wakanda" (talking drum, fula flute, optional trap)**

*Tempo and key*
- Tempo 112–132 BPM, default 120.
- "Killmonger/trap" sub-mode: same tempo range, half-time backbeat (snare or clap on beat 3).
- Meter 4/4, with an optional 12/8 shuffle for the hand drums.
- Key: minor by default (G, C or E minor).
- "Royal/reveal" sub-mode in major (C major default, following the "Wakanda" reading).

*Progressions*
- Drone: **i pedal with ♭VII/i and ♭VI/i upper alternation**.
- Brass and choir: **i – ♭VI – ♭VII – i**.
- Trap: **i – ♭II – i – ♭VII**.
- Major sub-mode: **I – ♭VII – IV – I**.

*Parts*
- **Lead:** fula-flute-like, G4–D6 (MIDI 67–86).
  - 3–6-note motifs centred on the tonic and fifth, in 2-bar call / 2-bar answer phrases.
  - Grace notes and pitch-bend scoops; occasional short high accented "shout" notes.
  - An intensity parameter adds saturation in FL Studio (Fruity Blood Overdrive, roughly mirroring Decapitator).
- **Counter-line/ostinato:** a "name-rhythm" talking-drum part.
  - Turn a user-entered word into hits: one hit per syllable, stressed syllable accented.
  - Two pitches a fourth or fifth apart, with a glide; repeat every bar.
  - Alternative: a kora-like 16th-note arpeggio, C4–C6.
- **Bass:** 808 sub, C1–G2 (MIDI 24–43). Notes fall on the hand-drum accents; glide to ♭VII/♭VI; long sustains in trap mode.
- **Drums:**
  - primary groove from interlocking 16th-note hand-drum parts (talking drum, djembe or sabar timbres; FPC or Slicex);
  - 808 kick doubling the accents;
  - trap mode adds 1/16 hats with 1/32 and triplet rolls and a half-time clap.
- **Chords:** brass (horns/trombones C3–C5) and choir "ah" pads in whole notes.

*Build:* solo flute or voice over a drone (4–8 bars), then the talking-drum motif, then 808 and hats, then brass and choir tutti.

**Variant B: "Mandalorian" (bass recorder, synths, heartbeat pulse)**

*Tempo and key*
- Tempo 70–84 BPM, default 75. Percussion may run on a 16th grid (felt as about 150).
- Meter 4/4.
- Key: C minor by default, D minor as an alternative.
- Scale: natural minor plus half-whole-diminished colour tones (♭2, ♮3, ♯4/♭5, ♮6).

*Progressions*
- **i – ♭II – i – i** (tension neighbour).
- **i – ♭III – ♭V(♯IV) – i** (minor-third cycle from the octatonic set).
- **i – ♭VI – ♭VII – i** (heroic western climax).
- **i°7** as a passing chord.

*Parts*
- **Lead:** bass-recorder register, F3–G5 (MIDI 53–79).
  - Long held notes, short 2–4-note pickups, generous rests.
  - Dotted-1/8 delay (Fruity Delay 3) and long reverb.
- **Counter-line/ostinato:** guitar-synth or pluck 8th-note pulse on root and fifth, or a 16th-note synth arpeggio. Piano low octaves on downbeats.
- **Bass:** synth bass root pulse in 8ths, locked to the kick.
- **Drums:**
  - "heartbeat" kick (two close hits on beat 1 and on beat 3);
  - sparse hand drums and toms;
  - metallic clinks or anvil hits on off-beats in place of hi-hats;
  - snare rolls and cymbal swells into climaxes;
  - orchestral bass drum only at the climax.

*Build:* solo recorder with delay, then heartbeat and bass, then piano and guitar, then synths, then orchestra.

**Variant C: "Oppenheimer" (violin ostinato with accelerando)**

*Tempo and key*
- Start at 72–90 BPM, default 80.
- Accelerate in 8–21 steps to 2–3× (cap about 240), stepping whenever the ostinato changes direction.
- Simpler mode: a fixed tempo with note-value diminution (♩ → ♪ → triplet ♪ → 𝅘𝅥𝅯).
- Meter 3/4 by default, 4/4 as an alternative.
- Key: E minor by default (relative G major), alternatively D or B minor.
- Melody pitch set: **six notes, one diatonic degree omitted** (e.g., E F♯ G B C D).

*Progressions*
- **v – ♭VI** oscillation (Bm – C in E minor).
- **i – ♭VI – ♭III – ♭VII**.
- **i pedal with ♭VI alternation**.

*Parts*
- **Bass:** a 4-note cell in half or whole notes, E1–E3, repeated unchanged while everything above accelerates.
- **Lead:** solo violin, G3–E6 (MIDI 55–88).
  - A six-note cell, rising and falling.
  - "Romantic ↔ neurotic" macro: from legato with vibrato (mod wheel) to 16th spiccato and 32nd tremolo with wide pitch-bend glissandi.
- **Counter-line/ostinato:** second violin or viola running 16th-note scalar up-down figures inside the six-note set. A counter-melody enters only after the lead is established.
- **Drums:** off by default. Optional low synth sub-booms on phrase starts or one timpani-like hit at the peak.
- **Orchestration:** strings plus synth pads. A brass or brass-synth swell enters after about 16–20 bars. Minimal woodwinds.

*Build:* bass cell, then lead, then counter-melody, then brass swell, then accelerating ostinato climax, then an abrupt cut to silence.

**Optional modifiers (apply to any variant)**
- **"Inversion" (Tenet):**
  - add the retrograde of each motif (or generate palindromic motifs);
  - reverse-swell velocity ramps ending in hard cuts;
  - a 16th-note synth-bass pulse on a tonic pedal;
  - breath and heartbeat sample lanes;
  - standalone tempo 120–140 BPM. This is unsourced; there is no Tenet BPM data.
- **"Gym-Hop" (Creed/Gambino):**
  - hip-hop kit built from found sounds (punches and rope slaps on kick and snare);
  - tempo 80–90 BPM half-time or 120–130 BPM;
  - G or E♭ minor;
  - bass oscillating **i – ♭II** (Phrygian);
  - a triplet counter-line over a straight-16th rhythm guitar or hat;
  - 16-bar organic intro, then the beat drop, with orchestra and choir stabs on top.
