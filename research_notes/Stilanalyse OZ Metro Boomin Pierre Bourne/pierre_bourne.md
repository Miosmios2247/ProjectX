# Pi'erre Bourne (Jordan Jenks): production style for a style-based MIDI idea generator

> How these notes were made (researched 2026-11-03): direct page fetches were blocked in the research sandbox. en.wikipedia.org, thefader.com and the Wikipedia mirror wiki.krisyotam.com did not resolve. Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from a full read of each page. Quotes are as short as the extracts showed them. BPM and key values come from automated analysers (Tunebat, Musicstax, Songdata and SongBPM, all derived from Spotify; also GetSongBPM, AcousticBrainz, Mixmeister). These tools often swap relative major and minor keys and report half-time or double-time tempos. Lines marked [INF] are my own inferences or proposed generator defaults, not sourced facts.

## 1. Best-known productions: verified credits, tempo and key

### Takeaway
Three of the example tracks in the brief are not Pi'erre Bourne productions. "Shoota" is credited to Maaly Raw, "Location" to Harry Fraud, and "Rich Nigga Shit" to Metro Boomin (with additional production by Peter Lee Johnson). The verified Bourne productions ("Magnolia", "wokeuplikethis*", most of *Die Lit*, and his own "Sossboy", "Poof" and "4U") mostly sit at 140–163 BPM when counted full-time (70–82 half-time), with 163 BPM recurring across the *Die Lit* era. Detected keys split roughly evenly between major and minor, which is unusual for trap.

  - Proposed generator default: 150–163 BPM.
  - Optional presets: a "Die Lit" preset at 163 BPM and a "Life of Pi'erre" preset at 140–153 BPM. Allow 105–125 BPM only as an "R&B/slow" option ("4U", "Foreign").

## 2. Melodic and harmonic signatures

### Takeaway
Critics consistently describe Bourne's melodic language as "airy video-game melodies" and "neon-smeared synths" over classic trap drums. The available transcriptions show short diatonic loops of two to four chords: natural minor with a minor v ("Magnolia": Bm–Em–F#m, or Bm–G–Gmaj7–Em) and two-chord oscillations ("wokeuplikethis*": Db–Fm).

### Cited Findings
- Pitchfork's Alphonse Pierre described Bourne's productions as "beats that pair airy video-game melodies with classic trap drums" — [Wikipedia: Pi'erre Bourne (quoting Pitchfork)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi%27erre_Bourne)
- The FADER describes his signature sound as "double kick drums, video-game reminiscent melodies, and neon-smeared synths" — [The FADER, "The life of Pi'erre Bourne" (2018)](https://thefader.com/2018/12/04/pierre-bourne-the-life-of-pierre-magnolia-interview)
- Pitchfork's "Pi'erre Bourne's 10 Best Beats" (April 2020) calls "Magnolia" possibly his signature beat. It says the melody has a video-game flavour and the bounce blends the places he has lived: New York, Atlanta and South Carolina — [Yahoo syndication of Pitchfork list](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/pi-erre-bourne-10-best-130000523.html)
- Hooktheory, Magnolia: key B minor, 163 BPM. The three most important chords sit on scale degrees 1, 4 and 5 and are all minor (Bm, Em, F#m) — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/playboi-carti/magnolia)
- Chordify, Magnolia (2017 release): Bm, G, Gmaj7, Em at 167 BPM. Other user transcriptions differ, for example "Bm, Em, D, E" — [Chordify](https://chordify.net/chords/playboi-carti-songs/magnolia-chords); [Chordify alt](https://chordify.net/chords/playboi-carti-magnolia-hide-in-my-sock-rap)
- Chordify, wokeuplikethis*: F minor reading, with a progression built around Db and Fm — [Chordify](https://chordify.net/chords/playboi-carti-feat-lil-uzi-vert-songs/wokeuplikethis-chords)
- Video-game influence:

- Bells as a melodic layer: a review of his EP *Grails* notes that on "NY in June" the drums, the bells and "ugh!" sounds sit alongside auto-tuned vocals — [Our Generation Music](https://ourgenerationmusic.com/new-music-spotlight/pierre-bourne-ep-grails/)
- Lighter textures in his solo work:

### Inferences
- [INF] Harmony in "Magnolia" mode: diatonic natural minor (Aeolian) with a minor v and no harmonic-minor leading tone.
  - Loops: i–iv–v (Hooktheory), or i–VI–VImaj7–iv (Chordify).
  - Add maj7 colour on VI; m7 and add9 voicings are acceptable (pluggnb lineage).
- [INF] Harmony in "bright" mode: major-key loops built from IV and vi, e.g. the two-chord IV–vi rocking in "wokeuplikethis*" (Db–Fm in Ab).
  - Keep loops to 2–4 chords, one chord per bar or two.
  - Prefer sustained pads, flutes or e-piano block chords over busy comping.
- [INF] Lead loop: a short 1–2-bar motif in a high register (about C5–C7), built from stepwise diatonic or pentatonic notes with occasional 4th/5th leaps.
  - Short, game-like note values, repeated with small rhythmic variations over 4–8 bars.
  - Avoid chromaticism. The "airy video-game" quality suggests square/pulse or FM-bell tones and generous space.
- [INF] Counter-melody: bells or a flute-like pluck answering the lead in its gaps (call and response), an octave above or below, possibly entering after the first 4 or 8 bars.
- [INF] "Lydian" and "Kirby-like" descriptors from the brief: I found no source for either. Do not hard-code Lydian. If wanted, offer it only as an optional "brightness" variant (raised 4th over the major tonic).

## 3. Sounds, synths, presets and DAW

### Takeaway
FL Studio is his origin DAW. Other plugin and preset claims come almost entirely from Equipboard (crowd-sourced, mostly from tutorial remakes and screenshots): FM8 on "Magnolia", Sylenth1, u-he ACE, Zebra2 and TyrellN6 (stock presets), the GForce Oddity2 "Academia Lead" preset, Massive, Absynth 5 and Omnisphere 2. No primary interview found here names the "Magnolia" synth.

### Cited Findings
- FL Studio:

## 4. Drums, 808s and the producer tag

### Takeaway
Sources agree on "classic trap drums" with bouncy 808s, crisp hi-hats and a signature use of double kicks. Hi-hats are kept relatively simple, with rolls and percussion used as accents, and the 808 is written as part of the rhythm rather than as held roots (this last point comes from lower-reliability tutorials). His tag, "Yo Pi'erre, you wanna come out here?", is a Jamie Foxx line from *The Jamie Foxx Show*. Describe it only; never reproduce it.

### Cited Findings

- "Classic trap drums" — [Wikipedia: Pi'erre Bourne (quoting Pitchfork)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi%27erre_Bourne). Other coverage summarises his sound as bouncy 808s, crisp hi-hats and airy melodic synths — [Observer (Case Western)](https://observer.case.edu/the-life-and-legacy-of-pierre-bourne-architect-of-a-new-trap-sound/)

### Inferences
- [INF] Drum defaults, in 4/4 at 140–163 BPM full-time:
  - **Snare/clap**: on beat 3 of each bar (the half-time backbeat).
  - **Kick**: on beat 1, plus syncopated pickups. Use deliberate kick doubles (two 16ths in a row, e.g. beat 1 and the "e" of 1, or the "a" of 2 and beat 3) as the signature.
  - **Hats**: straight 8ths, or 16ths in places. Add 16th-triplet or 32nd rolls only at phrase ends or every second bar, not continuously. Keep velocity variation moderate.
  - **Percussion**: one or two sparse perc or rim hits as answers to the melody.
- [INF] 808 defaults: follow the chord roots, but use a rhythmic pattern of its own that partly locks to the kick (often hitting with the kick, sometimes offset), with short notes and gaps. Add occasional octave jumps or slides at bar ends. Aim for "bounce" rather than sustained drones.
- [INF] Tag handling: leave a 1–2 bar "tag slot" before the downbeat of the intro, silent or filled with the user's own tag. Never synthesise or imitate the Jamie Foxx line.

### Inferences
- [INF] Generator arrangement defaults:
  1. Optional tag slot (silent or the user's own tag).
  2. A 4–8-bar intro with lead and pads only, or the lead alone, with a filtered or no-drum texture.
  3. Full drums and 808 on the downbeat of the hook.
  4. Sections in 8- and 16-bar blocks built from one 4- or 8-bar loop.

## 6. Interviews on workflow, what makes the sound recognisable, transcriptions and chord charts

### Takeaway
Bourne's process is fast, high-volume and instinctive: about 10 beats a day, beats made in cars ("Magnolia" in a friend's Mustang), working "from scratch" and trusting his gut.
