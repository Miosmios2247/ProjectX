## 1. Who is OZ? Bio, career, collaborators, loop/sample packs, DAW and tools

### Takeaway
OZ (Ozan Yıldırım, born 1992) is a Swiss producer of Turkish descent from Wattwil in the Toggenburg region. He taught himself, works in FL Studio (Wikipedia also lists keyboard), and runs a home studio in Wald (ZH). He started making beats as a hobby in 2008 and broke into US rap by e-mailing beat packs to artists, starting with Meek Mill. Since then he has had several Hot 100 No. 1s with Travis Scott and Drake. I found no official OZ loop or sample pack and no documented plugin list.

### Inferences
- [INF] He is self-taught, cannot read notation and writes melodies in FL Studio's piano roll. An OZ-style generator should therefore favour simple, pattern-based, diatonic writing (few chords, lots of repetition, small variations) over theory-heavy voice leading.
- [INF] His breakthrough came from e-mailing packs of finished beats (10 beats via WeTransfer; "Fair Trade" was sent "as part of a pack", see section 5). That suggests high output of short, self-contained, artist-ready loops. The generator should offer many short loop variants rather than one long through-composed piece.

## 2. Best-known productions (credits verified) with tempo and key

### Takeaway
The verified core corpus is SICKO MODE (Part II), Highest in the Room, Toosie Slide, Life Is Good, Popstar/Greece, the four Certified Lover Boy tracks, Meltdown, First Person Shooter and What Did I Miss?. Tempos fall into two readings of the same feel: half-time around 76–87 BPM, or double-time around 142–168 BPM. Keys lean towards minor (G minor, D minor, F minor, G♯/C♯ minor). Automated key detectors often disagree, so treat key data as approximate. "Chicago Freestyle" and "Laugh Now Cry Later" are **not** OZ productions.

## 3. Melodic and harmonic signatures

### Takeaway
The available evidence (Hooktheory plus several user-submitted chord sheets) points to plain natural-minor (Aeolian) harmony with a minor v. Typical shapes are a two-chord i–v vamp (Highest in the Room), i–iv–v with all chords minor (Girls Want Girls), and VI–(v)–VII–i loops (Toosie Slide). Arrangements are sparse and "airy", and the melody is a simple, repeated hook-loop. The "unusual" quality OZ claims seems to come from timbre (a strange sound layer), not from harmonic dissonance.

### Cited Findings
- **Girls Want Girls:** Hooktheory gives the key as G minor and says the three most important chords sit on scale degrees 1, 4 and 5, all minor (Gm, Cm, Dm), i.e. i–iv–v in natural minor. — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/drake-feat-lil-baby/girls-want-girls)
- **Highest in the Room:** chord sheets centre on Dm–Am (i–v), with C and B♭ also appearing. — [ChordU](https://chordu.com/chords-tabs-travis-scott-highest-in-the-room-id_tfSS1e3kYeo). Intro Dm/F–Fmaj7, verses and chorus cycling Dm and Am/D, a B♭ section near the end. — [GuitarTabsExplorer](https://www.guitartabsexplorer.com/scott-travis/highest-in-the-room-chords). Outro B♭–Gm–Dm–Am. — [Chordify (outro)](https://chordify.net/chords/travis-scott-highest-in-the-room-outro-only-designed-lizard). Simplest reading Dm–Am–Am–Dm. — [Chordify](https://chordify.net/chords/travis-scott-songs/highest-in-the-room-chords). Detectors conflict: Tunebat says G minor. — [Tunebat](https://tunebat.com/Info/HIGHEST-IN-THE-ROOM-Travis-Scott/3eekarcy7kvN4yt5ZFzltW)

- **Toosie Slide:** chord sheets list E7, D♯m7, F♯, G♯m7. — [Chordify via Pinterest](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/chords-for-drake-toosie-slide-e7-dm7-f-gm7-play-along-with-guitar-ukulele-or-piano-with-interactive-chords-and--397442735869291456/). ### Inferences
- [INF] **Default scale:** natural minor (Aeolian) with a *minor* v. Both clearly sourced progressions (GWG i–iv–v all minor; HITR Dm–Am) avoid the harmonic-minor major V and leading tone. Allow the raised 7th only rarely, as colour (≤10% of loops).
- [INF] **Progression pool (minor-key degrees):**
  - P1 `i – v` (2-bar or 4-bar vamp; from HITR)
  - P2 `i – iv – v – v` or `i – iv – v – i` (from GWG Hooktheory)
  - P3 `VI – v – VII – i` (Toosie chord sheets read in G♯ minor: E–D♯m–F♯–G♯m)
  - P4 `VI – VII – i – i` (Toosie chorus E–F♯–G♯m. GWG guitar sheets give the same shape after capo conversion, see section 6)
  - P5 `VI – iv – i – v` (HITR outro B♭–Gm–Dm–Am)
  - P6 optional one-chord `i` vamp with melodic movement (not sourced, a plausible minimal variant)
- [INF] **Colour:** add 7ths (m7 on i and v, maj7 on VI and III) in about 40% of chords, and occasional first-inversion voicings (cf. "Dm/F", "Fmaj7" in the HITR intro sheet). Keep voicings close, 3–4 notes, C3–C5.
- [INF] **Loop length:** 4 bars at one chord per bar as the default, or a 2-bar `i–v` vamp repeated. The hook is 8 bars (loop ×2).
- [INF] **Melody/lead:** a short motif of 1–2 bars repeated, with variation only in the last bar of the loop (call/answer). Mostly chord tones plus natural-minor passing tones, about a 1.5-octave range in C4–C6, longer notes landing on chord changes. It should sound like an improvised, loopable guitar or pluck idea (HITR origin), not a composed tune.
- [INF] **Counter-melody:** one "odd" signature layer (see section 4) that answers the lead sparsely, with 1–3 notes per bar, glides or long tones, and a higher register (C5–C7), entering at the hook.
- [INF] **Dissonance:** keep harmonic dissonance low. Get the "unusual" effect from timbre, detuning and FX on the counter layer, not from chromatic harmony.

### Gaps
- No source gives explicit loop lengths (2 vs 4 bars), arpeggio use, octave doubling or register for OZ's loops.
- No source discusses Phrygian, Dorian or harmonic-minor use by OZ. The Toosie Slide "C♯ minor" detector reading would imply Dorian colours (major IV F♯), but chord sheets favour G♯ Aeolian. Unresolved.
- I found no Hooktheory TheoryTab for Highest in the Room, Toosie Slide or Life Is Good.

## 5. Workflow, loop exchange with co-producers, drums/808 conventions, arrangement and recognisability

### Takeaway
OZ works remotely from his Swiss home studio in a loop economy, and loops travel in both directions. Collaborators (Nik D, Ambezza, Cubeatz) send him melodic loops, and he adds trap drums and pitches the beat to artists (HITR, Life Is Good). He also supplies melody-only loops that drum-focused producers finish (No Friends in the Industry with Vinylz), and sometimes he does everything himself (Toosie Slide, made in about a day). The drums documented on his records are hi-hat-heavy trap kits (hats, snare or claps, kicks placed against the melodic hook). Arrangements often involve beat switches and outros finished by others (Mike Dean). I found no OZ-specific 808 information.

### Inferences
- [INF] **Degree translation for the generator (no melodies reproduced):**
  - HITR in D minor: `i – v` (Dm–Am), intro `i/♭3 – IIImaj7` (Dm/F–Fmaj7), outro `VI – iv – i – v` (B♭–Gm–Dm–Am).
  - Toosie Slide in G♯ minor: `VI(7) – v7 – VII – i7` (E–D♯m7–F♯–G♯m7), chorus `VI – VII – i`.
  - GWG in G minor: either `i – iv – v` (Hooktheory) or, converting the capo-3 guitar shapes C–D–Em to sounding E♭–F–Gm, `VI – VII – i`. The two readings may describe different sections.
- [INF] Common thread: the Aeolian `VI – VII – i` cadence (Toosie, and possibly GWG) and minor-v vamps (HITR, GWG). These are good primary templates for an "OZ" chord generator.
- [INF] The "E7" in the Toosie chart is probably an auto-detection artefact for Emaj7 or E(add9), because a dominant E7 (with D natural) falls outside G♯ minor. Use maj7 on VI.
